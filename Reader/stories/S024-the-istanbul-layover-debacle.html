The Istanbul Layover Debacle

9 minute read · 41 illustrations
In this story
  1. Lost in Translation
  2. Turkish Lounge: The Land of Indulgence
  3. The Shower Fiasco
  4. The Olive Heist

The business class experience had barely worn off when the trio touched down in Istanbul for their layover. The airport was enormous, bustling with travelers from all over the world. The air smelled of strong Turkish coffee and sizzling kebabs, and the chatter around them was a mix of languages that neither Heather nor Buddy understood.

Neet, of course, was unfazed. “Alright, team. We have five hours in this city. Time to dominate.”

Buddy sighed. “Neet, let's just eat something and nap in the lounge.”

Neet scoffed. “Where's your sense of adventure? This place is teeming with opportunity.”

“Mine has a footrest,” Buddy said.

Heather stopped beside a departure display and checked their boarding time. Then she looked down at them, smiling despite the long journey.

“Food, showers, and somewhere to sit. That sounds wonderful.”

Buddy liked this plan. Three things, all inside one room. Neet was already looking down a corridor that appeared to contain another hundred rooms.

Lost in Translation

Heather approached an airport information desk where a stern-looking Turkish man stood behind the counter. She attempted a polite inquiry.

“Hi! Do you know where the transfer lounge is?”

The man frowned. “Lounge? No.”

Heather blinked. “Umm… transfer? Rest area?”

The man shook his head. “No English.”

Buddy decided to help. Words weren't working, but everyone knew what being tired looked like. He stepped into the man's view, folded his legs beneath him, and rested his chin on his paws.

The man leaned over the counter to see what he was doing. Buddy closed his eyes. Then opened one to see whether the demonstration had succeeded.

Neet peered down at him.

“He thinks you've arrived.”

Buddy lifted his head and gave an enormous yawn. Surely that clarified the request.

Before Heather could try again, Neet jumped onto the counter and barked confidently. “LOUNGE. FOOD. NAP. NOW.”

The man looked from Neet to Heather. She gathered Neet off the counter before he could repeat himself.

“Sorry,” she said. “He thinks volume is a language.”

Another airport employee walked past and overheard the commotion. He turned and simply pointed in a direction, muttering something in rapid Turkish.

Heather followed his hand with her eyes, then pointed the same way herself. He nodded. She thanked both men and set Neet down beside her.

Buddy stood up. His demonstration had mostly collected a little dust on his beard. Heather brushed it off with her fingers before they left.

Neet grinned. “See? You just gotta speak their language.”

Buddy muttered, “I think he was just sick of you barking.”

The man behind the desk raised a hand as Heather waved goodbye. Neet raised a paw back.

“International relations,” he said.

“Keep walking,” Buddy said.

Turkish Lounge: The Land of Indulgence

Once inside the Istanbul Airport Lounge, it was clear that this was no ordinary airport pit stop. There were buffet stations overflowing with fresh food, showers, and even a sleeping area with cozy recliners.

Neet's eyes gleamed. “This is heaven.”

Buddy immediately headed for a recliner, curling up before Neet could rope him into more chaos. He chose the one facing away from the buffet. If he couldn't see a new idea forming, perhaps it would leave him alone.

Neet stood beside the chair, nose aimed toward the food. Buddy lowered his own nose between his paws.

Heather put their bags beside the chairs and sat for a moment. Somewhere behind Buddy, a plate clinked. He lifted one ear.

“We'll eat after I freshen up,” she said. “I'm going to take a shower. You two behave.”

Neet watched her head toward the shower rooms. Buddy heard his little feet follow.

He opened his eyes.

“I think she meant here.”

Neet was already gone.

The Shower Fiasco

Heather walked into one of the private shower rooms, sighing as she turned on the warm water. However, as she reached for the soap, she noticed something was… off.

The shower door was clear glass. The room itself was private, but Neet had followed her in and was inspecting it with unnerving interest. She put him outside the shower room and closed its outer door.

She had just stepped back under the water when a loud scratching began at that door.

“HEATHER!” Neet's voice echoed. “HOW DOES THE WATER PRESSURE FEEL?”

“NEET, GO AWAY!”

“ARE THERE BUTTONS?!”

Buddy lifted his head from the nearby recliner. “Neet. She closed the door.” He stayed awake until Neet backed away; he wanted Heather to finish so they could all rest.

“You can inspect the chair,” Buddy offered.

Neet looked toward him.

“Silently. From the other chair.”

Neet took two steps toward it, then noticed the towel counter beside the shower rooms. White towels sat in a neat stack on its low shelf. All this luxury, and nobody was wearing any of it.

He pulled the top towel down by a corner. It unfolded onto the floor. Neet ducked beneath an edge and turned until the towel draped over his back, with its ends hanging beside his front legs.

Buddy watched the white shape advance toward the counter.

“Did you have a shower?”

“No. This is the part afterward.”

Heather, now red with embarrassment, scrambled to finish her shower and threw her clothes back on. She stepped out, only to find Neet standing at the counter with the stolen towel wrapped around his tiny body.

Heather groaned. “What… what are you doing?”

Neet smirked. “Embracing the luxury life.”

He lifted his chin. The towel slid over his left front paw. He lowered his chin and stood very still.

Heather crouched to free the paw. “Luxury has got you.”

Neet tried to turn toward Buddy. The towel tightened under his right front paw.

“Stay there,” Heather said, and lifted it clear of both paws. Neet emerged with his ears upright, as though he had arranged the entire demonstration.

She returned the towel to the attendant for laundering before luxury could get him again.

Buddy climbed down from his recliner. Heather's hair was still damp, but she looked refreshed. She ran a hand over his head, then Neet's.

“Now we can eat.”

Neet started toward the buffet.

“With me,” she added.

He stopped and waited for her, wearing nothing but an expression of expensive patience.

The Olive Heist

After their chaotic lounge experience, they moved to the dining section where an entire counter was dedicated to olives. Green, black, stuffed, spiced—every kind imaginable.

Heather offered Neet an olive from her plate. He took one bite and gasped. “We must take these home.”

Heather gave him a warning look. “Neet, we're not smuggling food.”

Buddy sniffed the choices. Now that he was properly awake, eating something seemed too modest an ambition. He wanted to find the best thing on this magnificent counter.

“How do you know that's the best one?” he asked Neet.

Neet glanced at the other bowls. “There are better ones?”

“We should compare.”

Heather chose a few different olives and carried the plate to their table, bringing their bags over too. Buddy climbed onto a padded seat beside it, ready to supervise his experiment.

Neet sat on the neighboring chair. “Which first?”

“The green one.”

Neet ate it.

“And?”

“Excellent.”

Buddy tried one from the same little group, then looked at the black ones. Perhaps those would be better. He nudged one green olive away from the others with his nose so they could remember which kind they had already tried.

Neet ate that one too.

“That was the marker.”

“It was excellent.”

Buddy looked at the empty spot on the plate. He hadn't anticipated needing to protect the evidence from his fellow investigator.

Heather took another small serving for them. Buddy watched her set it down and began again. Green ones to the left, black ones to the right. Stuffed ones in the middle. He moved his nose carefully between them.

A round olive rolled across the plate and joined the wrong group.

Buddy nudged it back. Neet leaned forward.

“No. I'm arranging those.”

“Then which can I eat?”

Buddy looked from one little pile to another. He had sorted the meal so thoroughly that none of it was available to eat.

Heather laughed softly and drew one olive out of his rows.

“Try this, Buddy.”

He ate it. It was splendid. He nosed another toward her, abandoning the system long enough to share his discovery.

She took it with her fork. “Oh, that one's lovely.”

Buddy sat taller. His experiment had finally produced something useful.

Neet ate one from the abandoned rows.

“Also excellent.”

“They don't all have to win,” Buddy said.

“Why not?”

Buddy considered the plate. Then he ate another. The argument was becoming difficult to sustain.

Heather settled into her own meal. For a few minutes, Neet concentrated on food instead of acquiring the furniture, and Buddy stopped trying to turn the meal into a filing system. Heather's shoulders eased. She caught them watching her and smiled.

“Yes, I'm enjoying myself too.”

Buddy finished eating and turned around on the padded seat. He had found excellent food and was still allowed to lie down afterward. Perhaps he could enjoy both without further research.

He settled beside the table and closed his eyes.

Neet looked at the olives remaining on their plate. The problem with finding so many winners was leaving them behind.

Heather was checking their boarding time. Neet grabbed a clean napkin from the table, carefully wrapped up a handful of olives, and shoved the bundle under Buddy's sleeping body.

Buddy, half-conscious, grumbled. “Mmm. Why does my stomach feel… lumpy?”

Neet hushed him. “You're an accomplice now.”

Buddy opened one eye. “I applied to be a pillow.”

He shifted to improve the situation. The bundle slid toward the edge of the cushion.

Neet caught its loose corner in his teeth before it fell. He put it down, then nudged the bundle back underneath Buddy.

Buddy lifted his chin. “My pillow is moving.”

“Settling.”

Buddy's chin sank again. He should have chosen a simpler meal.

The boarding announcement came while Heather was gathering their bags. Buddy stood, revealing the napkin.

Neet scooped it into his little bag and strutted toward the gate before Heather saw it. Buddy watched Neet's little bag. He was wide awake now.

“Those are the leftovers.”

“The winners.”

“You don't need to award them a flight.”

Heather turned back to count her companions. Neet fell into step beside her, looking helpful. Buddy came up on her other side. She rested a hand briefly on his back as they joined the flow of travelers.

Buddy yawned. “If I get detained for smuggling produce, I will haunt you.”

Heather sighed. “I don't know how we made it this far without being permanently banned from international travel.”

Neet grinned, popping one last olive into his mouth. “Talent.”
