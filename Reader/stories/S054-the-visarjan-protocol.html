The Visarjan Protocol

The days of Ganesh Chaturthi passed in a rhythm the dogs had begun to know. Morning aartis, evening lamps, Buddy’s squeaky-bone offerings, Neet’s military “quality control” — the Patel household had lived in glow and laughter.

But every arrival has a farewell, and today was Visarjan Day.

Heather woke early, tying her hair back, her face set with quiet determination. Sagar brewed chai with extra cardamom, his calm masking the tug of goodbye.

The generals, however, were in full crisis mode.

Morning Security Briefing

Neet paced the living room like a field marshal. “Today we escort the VIP out. Maximum vigilance. Minimal sloppiness. No unauthorized squirrels.”

Buddy nosed his blanket toward the shrine. “But… but I don’t want him to goooo. Can’t we just… hide him? In the closet? Under my blanket?!”

Neet glared. “Ridiculous. He’s the Lord of Obstacles. He’d find his way out of your blanket in two seconds.”

Buddy put one paw on the blanket. “Then can’t we adopt him? Like permanent roommate?”

“He already has somewhere to live.”

“So did you. You still sleep on my pillow.”

Neet resumed pacing, taking a slightly wider route around the blanket.

He sighed. “This is the way. Duty fulfilled. Mission complete. Then he departs.”

Buddy whimpered. “You’re too calm about this. I’m devastated.”

Neet glanced at him, softer now. “I’ll miss him too.”

Buddy looked up at the flowers. Yesterday he had known where everything belonged: the bone at Bappa’s feet, the lamp, the songs, Neet pretending he wasn’t listening from his sunbeam. By tonight there would be a space where their guest had been.

“What if I forget?”

“Forget what?”

“The smell. Or how he looked when Heather was singing.”

Neet stopped pacing. Buddy lowered his nose to a loose marigold petal on the floor. It smelled of flowers and the place where everyone had gathered. He nudged it onto his blanket.

“Taking that?” Neet asked.

“Emergency remembering.”

Buddy found another petal, then a third. He nosed them into a small pile and sniffed deeply to check how much of the festival he had collected.

He sneezed.

The pile scattered. One petal landed on Neet’s paw.

“Memories escaping,” Neet said.

“Hold that one!”

Neet remained still while Buddy gathered the other two. Then Buddy reached for the petal on his paw, breathed in too enthusiastically, and sneezed again.

Heather looked over from the shrine. “What have you got there?”

Buddy stood over the surviving petal. It had already endured two disasters. He was not letting it endure a broom.

Heather knelt and folded a corner of the blanket over it. “There. You can keep that one.”

Buddy nosed the fold closed. Neet inspected it without opening it.

“One item,” he said. “Travel light.”

“There’s a lot on it.”

The Preparations

Heather prepared the farewell plate: coconuts, turmeric, flowers, and modaks. She draped the murti with a fresh garland. The air shimmered with incense.

Buddy circled the plate, whispering, “If he doesn’t finish these modaks, I volunteer as tribute.”

“Not approved,” Neet snapped.

Buddy sat down beside the plate.

“I can wait for a vacancy.”

Heather moved the plate onto the table. Buddy watched it rise, taking his prospects with it.

Buddy returned to the shrine. His bone was still at Bappa’s feet, the good squeaking end pointing toward their guest. He picked it up and took it to his folded blanket.

Neet watched him set it down. “That too?”

“He knows it.”

Heather gathered the blanket for the car and tucked the bone inside. Buddy followed closely enough that she had to pause to let him get out from under it.

“Your luggage is coming, sweetheart.”

Sagar lifted the idol carefully, holding it with reverence. Heather carried the plate of offerings. Together they moved toward the door, the dogs padding alongside like royal guards. Buddy kept looking back toward the shrine.

Neet slowed until he was beside him.

“Guest is this way,” he murmured.

Buddy turned forward. He wanted to go with him.

At the threshold, Heather whispered: “Ganpati Bappa Morya.”

Neet barked solemnly once. Buddy tried to howl but his voice cracked into a squeaky hiccup.

The Royal Escort

The car ride was unlike any other. Sagar drove carefully, Heather beside him steadying Ganpati amid the flowers.

Neet sat upright in the back, eyes scanning the horizon like a Secret Service agent. “Perimeter clear. All safe.”

Buddy leaned toward Heather’s lap, where she steadied the idol. “Are you comfortable, Bappa? Do you need water? Snacks? A blanket?”

Neet checked Buddy’s blanket, then looked back out the window. “He has everything.”

Buddy tucked the loose corner in. A moment later he untucked it.

“What now?”

“What if he’s too warm?”

Neet turned from the window and helped flatten the corner.

Buddy immediately lifted it again.

“The petal.”

“It’s still inside.”

“Now it’s flat.”

“It was flat before.”

Buddy pressed his nose into the fold. The bone shifted beneath the blanket and squeaked. Neet’s head snapped around; Heather laughed softly.

“Everything all right back there?”

Buddy looked up. While he had been checking his luggage, the sun had reached Bappa’s face. Heather tilted her head toward the murti, giving him a clear view.

“There he is, Buddy.”

Buddy left the fold alone.

Buddy whispered: “He’s leaving forever. I have to make sure he’s cozy.”

From the idol came the faintest twinkle in the sunlight, as if Ganesha was smiling at both of them.

At the Water’s Edge

They reached the lake where families had gathered for immersion. Drums echoed, chants of “Ganpati Bappa Morya!” filled the air, children ran with sparklers.

Buddy’s wagging stopped for one startled beat, then resumed twice as fast. His body seemed to have joined the drums without consulting the rest of him.

“Formation,” Neet whispered.

“I’m in it.”

“You’re vibrating.”

“So are the drums.”

Neet faced the water again. Buddy had wanted a slow, quiet goodbye he could keep exactly as it was. Instead there were flowers moving, voices rising, sunlight everywhere. He could barely decide what to look at.

The Patel family stepped forward. Sagar and Heather carried the idol together. The dogs walked at their sides — Neet stiff with solemnity, Buddy wagging furiously at every drumbeat.

At the water’s edge, Heather’s eyes shimmered. “Every year I feel the same… I don’t want to let go.”

Sagar squeezed her hand. “But letting go is part of the prayer.”

Neet watched silently. Buddy whimpered. “This part’s the worst.”

Heather and Sagar lowered the idol gently into the water, flowers bobbing around it, the sunlight catching on the colors.

Together they said: “Ganpati Bappa Morya. Pudhchya Varshi Lavkar Ya.”

The Canine Farewell

As the murti drifted, the dogs sat at the shoreline.

Neet, voice steady: “Mission complete. VIP escorted successfully.”

Buddy, tears in his eyes: “Best guest ever.”

And then — a breeze swept across the lake, scattering marigold petals into the air. The petals swirled above the dogs before drifting onto the water.

A petal dipped toward Buddy’s nose. He lifted his head, mouth opening to catch it. This one had been right here, at the goodbye. It could join the one in his blanket.

Another floated past. Buddy shifted his paws, ready to follow.

Neet was still watching the water. His ears had lowered a little. Buddy stopped beside him. If he chased the petal, he would have to turn away.

He sat down again and let it pass. The two dogs watched the orange petals settle among the flowers, all the way until the ripples carried them farther out.

Buddy gasped. “He said goodbye to us!”

Neet blinked, his composure flickering. “Approved. Deeply approved.”

The Return Escort

On the walk back to the car, Neet kept his usual brisk pace for three steps, then waited for Buddy. This time he did not announce that anyone was delaying the mission.

Heather opened the car door and checked the blanket. The folded corner had come loose. One small petal lay flattened on the seat, beside the bone.

“Oh, your souvenir.” She laid it back in the fold.

Buddy sniffed it. It smelled faintly of marigold and strongly of Buddy.

“I sat on it,” he said.

Neet considered the damaged keepsake. “Securely transported.”

Buddy gave a small, watery snort. Heather rubbed behind his ears before climbing into her seat. He leaned into her hand for as long as she left it there.

The place beside her was different now. Buddy could see the flowers she still held and the curve of her wrist. He settled back onto the blanket, leaving the folded corner free.

Neet sat beside him. For a while he watched the road. Then he lay down too.

Evening Benediction

Back home, the shrine was empty, the marigold garlands wilting. But the house didn’t feel empty. It felt… full, as though Ganesha had left his laughter in the walls, his peace in the air.

Heather lit one small diya. “Even if he’s gone, he’s here.”

Sagar nodded. “Always.”

Heather set the blanket down beside Buddy. The petal slipped onto the floor. He sniffed it once, then looked toward the shrine.

Sagar sat nearby, resting a hand on Neet’s back. Heather settled beside him. Neither hurried the dogs away from the empty place.

Buddy drew the bone out of his blanket. It had gone with them and come home with them. He carried it back to where he had first left it.

Neet curled up in his usual sunbeam, Buddy dropped his squeaky toy in the shrine.

Buddy left the toy there and settled beside Neet. Neither of them moved it when the house grew quiet.
