Uttarayan - Day One

9 minute read · 34 illustrations

January in Gujarat had filled the sky with kites. By the time Heather, Neet and Buddy reached Ahmedabad for Uttarayan, Neet had tried to follow three of them from the train window.

The third had disappeared behind a building. Neet inspected the other window.

“It's outside,” Buddy said.

“I'm checking all exits.”

At the station, dhol drums beat somewhere beyond the crowd. The aroma of freshly fried fafda and jalebi reached Neet before Heather had lifted the last bag. He aimed his little orange head toward breakfast, then a cart of kites rolled past and he aimed it toward glory. His Dorito-shaped ears tried to attend to both.

“Buddy! Look at this place! It's a carnival of smells and sounds!”

“It's a little… overwhelming.” Buddy stayed close to Heather's leg. A kite on the cart bobbed above him. He ducked. The next one bobbed. He ducked again.

Heather waited for the cart to pass, her hand resting lightly on his back. “They've gone, love.”

Buddy straightened. Neet was still looking after the cart.

“Those birds are very flat,” Neet said.

“So is the train window. You didn't catch that either.”

Their host, Heather's friend Meera, welcomed them into her home and led them up to the rooftop terrace. All around them, colors tugged at the blue sky. Neighboring rooftops were crowded with people, and every so often a shout of “Kai po che!” crossed the narrow street.

Meera had stacked kites against a wall and set out spools of manjha, the special thread coated with glass powder to help cut other kites in the sky. She kept the thread beside her hands, away from the dogs. Neet had eyes only for the paper wings.

“This is it, Buddy! The battleground of champions! We're going to be sky warriors!”

Buddy peeked out from behind a spool. “I think I'll just watch from here.”

Meera picked it up. Buddy moved with its shadow for two steps, then discovered he had followed his hiding place into the middle of the terrace. He backed out again.

“You can have the sky,” he told Neet. “I'm finding somewhere to stand.”

Heather tied a small scarf around Neet's neck against the January breeze. He lifted his chin as though she were fastening a medal.

“You're going to love this. But no biting the kites.”

Neet looked over her shoulder at the stack.

“No,” she added.

He looked back at her with an injured expression. He had only been assessing them.

Meera offered Heather a scarlet kite with a yellow center. Heather held it up to the sunlight, smiling at the color shining through. Neet sniffed its lower point, sneezed, and retreated a pace.

“It's ready,” he declared.

“Did you just sneeze on our kite?” Buddy asked.

“A warning shot.”

Heather took the spool while Meera carried the kite into position. Neet planted himself beside Heather, certain that somebody ought to tell the sky what to do.

“Pull! Pull! Let it loose now! You're losing altitude, Heather!”

The kite had not left Meera's hands.

“Give us a moment, coach,” Heather said.

Meera lifted it. The scarlet shape fluttered, tipped sideways and skimmed down onto the tiles. Neet sprang after it, stopping just short when Heather said his name.

He put his nose under the paper's edge. It lifted. He lifted his nose farther. The whole kite slid over his face.

For a moment, the commander could see nothing but yellow.

Buddy watched him back out.

“Higher,” Neet said, with considerable dignity. “We need to start higher.”

Meera laughed and retrieved the kite. Heather rubbed a finger along the crease, then stood where Meera directed. This time she waited. The breeze lifted the edge of Meera's sleeve. The kite rose, shivered and climbed.

Neet barked twice. Excellent. They had understood his correction.

Buddy had found a sunny corner with a view of Heather. He inspected the warm tiles, checked that nobody needed to walk through him, and placed all four paws inside the patch.

“Perfect.”

He lay down. His nose reached the shadow.

Buddy got up and moved backward. Now his hindquarters were cold. He considered the patch from above, then found a longer stretch of sunshine beside Heather's empty chair.

There. The terrace had finally provided enough room for the whole dog.

Above him, Heather's kite sailed in a small, bright arc. Buddy could watch it without moving anything except his eyes. He could smell the food downstairs. Heather was close. Even the drums sounded better when he wasn't standing inside a crowd.

Neet began hopping on his hind legs for a better view.

“Pull! Let it loose! Pull!”

Heather let out a little thread. Neet hopped higher.

“You're going to run out of dog before we run out of sky,” she told him.

He landed at the edge of Buddy's sunshine. On the next hop, he landed farther in.

Buddy extended one foreleg into the remaining warmth. Neet's shadow jumped across it.

“Could you command from somewhere else?”

“I need the best view.”

“That's what everyone says before standing on me.”

Neet moved one pace to the left. Buddy lowered his chin. The sky could now conduct its business without involving his paws.

Then a stray kite floated down onto his sunbeam.

Buddy jumped back with a soft yelp. The kite settled, lifted a corner and settled again.

He looked at the rest of the terrace. There was quite a lot of it.

Meera gathered up the loose thread before returning to Heather's side. Buddy waited until she had cleared it away, then approached the paper. If he could move it two paw lengths, the warm tiles would be his again.

“It's just paper, Buddy,” Neet said, darting over. “If you're not going to fight it, I will.”

“I'm trying to—”

Buddy nudged an edge. It made a crisp, sudden crackle.

Neet pounced.

Buddy withdrew his nose. This was now a much louder way of moving paper than he had intended.

Heather laughed as Neet's tiny teeth tugged at the colorful kite. “Neet, leave that alone! We're supposed to fly them, not eat them.”

Neet lifted his head, carrying a corner. The rest stayed on the tiles.

“Got it.”

“Some of it,” Buddy said.

Heather took the piece from his mouth. A small scrap caught on his scarf as he turned, but he was too busy inspecting his victory to notice.

Buddy approached again. He nudged the untorn edge until the kite slid out of his sunny patch, then stepped back into the warmth.

Neet had won a battle. Buddy had cleared the floor.

“Leave that one for me,” Buddy said.

Neet looked at the crumpled kite beside the chair.

“All right. You can have it.”

Buddy closed his eyes. One had to be careful what one asked for.

A cheer from the next terrace brought his head up again. A large golden kite had entered their airspace. Its owner, a competitive neighbor, had already cut down half the kites in the sky. He waved across to Meera, then pointed at Heather's scarlet kite.

Meera waved back. “Oh, he wants this one.”

Neet's eyes narrowed.

“That one's mine. Heather, take the spool! We're bringing that bird down.”

Heather already held the spool. Neet checked, satisfied, and began directing her with renewed vigor.

The two kites drew closer. The golden one flashed bright, then dark as it turned. Heather shifted her feet. Her kite dipped. Meera said something quickly, and Heather steadied her hands.

“Up!” Neet barked. “Up! Don't let it get above us!”

Buddy stood. The corner behind the chair looked quieter. He could slip there, let the others shout, and wait for Heather to sit down.

But the scarlet kite was wobbling. Heather's mouth had drawn into a small, intent smile. She wanted this one too.

Buddy stepped out of his sunbeam.

Neet was about to hop again. Buddy leaned his shoulder gently against him. Neet landed on four paws, startled.

“What?”

“You're standing where she wants to step.”

Neet looked up. Heather moved backward, and he sidestepped with Buddy, still staring at the golden kite.

“I was making room.”

“Good. Make some over here.”

Buddy led him beside the chair, away from Heather's feet. He sat. Neet remained standing, but he stopped bouncing. The scrap on his scarf stirred against his chest.

Meera watched the sky. “Wait…”

Neet opened his mouth.

Buddy touched his shoulder with his nose.

For once, Neet waited too.

Heather let the thread run, then drew her hands back with a swift, controlled movement. Their kite's manjha cut through the golden kite's thread. The golden shape broke free and spiraled downward beyond the terrace.

The rooftops erupted.

“Kai po che!” Neet howled, his tiny nub twitching. “Victory is ours!”

Buddy forgot the chair. He stepped forward to watch the kite fall.

“You actually did it?” He gave one bark with the others, then looked mildly surprised at himself.

Meera grinned. “That one was Buddy.”

He closed his mouth. On the neighboring terrace, someone cheered again. Buddy opened it.

Just once more.

The golden kite's owner put a hand over his heart and bowed his head in an extravagant show of defeat. Then he grinned and reached for another kite. Heather laughed and waved. Neet waved his front paw without knowing quite what had been agreed.

“Of course we did! I'm a champion, Buddy. You've got to aim high.”

Buddy looked back at his sunny patch. It had moved while he was helping. The crumpled kite beside the chair was now enjoying the warmth.

He nudged it into the shade.

“Not you.”

As the sun began to set, the sky turned orange and pink around the last silhouettes of kites. Meera put the spools away. Heather sat on the terrace with a plate of sweet jalebi and a cup of chai, and both dogs came to her without checking where the sunshine had gone.

Buddy nibbled cautiously on a small piece of fafda. Neet devoured his share, then looked up to see whether the sky had noticed.

“This place is amazing,” he said. “I can't wait to see what tomorrow brings.”

Buddy leaned against Heather. “As long as tomorrow isn't as loud, I'll be happy.”

The scrap on Neet's scarf fluttered against Buddy's ear.

“You've still got a kite.”

Neet glanced down. “A trophy.”

Heather plucked it off and set it beside the plate. Neet watched her do it, then tucked himself against her other side. There was room for both dogs now; neither had to defend it.

Buddy rested his chin against her knee. Next time, he would choose a sunbeam with a roof.
