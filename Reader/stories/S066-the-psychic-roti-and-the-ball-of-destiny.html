The Psychic Roti and the Ball of Destiny

5 minute read · 3 illustrations

It was one of those golden afternoons where time seemed to slow down, and the air smelled faintly of toasted flour and quiet ambition. Heather was perched in the main living room, locked into one of her intense virtual classes, surrounded by neatly stacked notebooks and a laptop screen filled with very serious-looking charts and formulas.

Neet lay near the entrance to the kitchen, his eyes half-closed but his soul fully awake to the mysterious rhythms of the universe. Buddy lay directly under the ceiling fan. He had shifted twice to find the strongest draught and had no intention of moving again.

In the kitchen, a culinary masterpiece was unfolding.

Sagar, armed with nothing but a rolling pin and sheer confidence, was crafting what he proudly referred to as his signature rotis. The rotis weren’t always round. Sagar watched each one inflate as if it might be the exception.

And it was during this sacred ritual— when the first perfectly puffed roti emerged from the skillet like a hot air balloon taking flight— that something supernatural occurred.

Sagar looked around, narrowed his eyes, and in an almost imperceptible whisper— one so low it barely disturbed the air— he uttered,

“Hey, idiot… your roti is ready.”

Heather kept talking. Buddy kept cooling.

But Neet’s ears twitched. His eyes snapped open like a sleeper agent being activated after years in hibernation. He turned his head slowly, his gaze locking onto the roti. The rest of him was already making arrangements.

Sagar, holding up the warm, slightly uneven but gloriously puffy roti, gave a subtle wave.

Neet launched into action. He took the route through the living room at full speed, overshot the kitchen entrance, and skidded back into it sideways.

He arrived looking as though this had been the route all along.

Heather glanced up from explaining regression models.

“And that gives us a fairly predictable—”

Neet slid past.

“—outcome.”

Buddy watched from under the fan. He followed the roti with his eyes, then stretched one paw into the cool air.

“Bring it here,” he suggested. Nobody did.

Sagar, amused and emboldened, turned his attention to Buddy.

“Heyyyyy, Buddy!” he called out in the most animated, high-pitched, borderline embarrassing human-dog-talk voice.

No reaction.

Sagar tried again, crouching low, slapping his knees, and calling out like he was auditioning for National Geographic’s How to Summon a Wild Beast.

Buddy lifted his head slightly, let out a long, mournful sigh as if summoned to carry the burdens of the world, and then promptly flopped back down like a soggy pancake.

Sagar resorted to desperate measures. “Hey, Buddy! Look, look! Roti!” He waved the bread dramatically, like a man trying to signal ships at sea.

Buddy stretched into a cooler strip of tile without getting up. The afternoon was improving.

Neet, already nibbling at his prize, looked back at Buddy with a subtle smirk that only a telepathic, roti-powered genius could deliver.

Heather, now completely unable to keep a straight face during her lecture, muted her mic and burst into laughter.

By evening, even Buddy was ready to move. Sagar chose that moment to unveil something extraordinary.

A new ball.

But this wasn’t just any ball. It was hollow and mesh-like, soft enough to catch, with a bounce neither dog had accounted for.

He rolled it onto the floor.

Neet’s eyes went wide. He put a paw on the ball to establish ownership. It shot out from under him.

Ownership would have to be established at speed.

He pounced, bounced it up with his nose, caught it mid-air, and flung himself onto the couch. A pillow fell. Neet bounced down after the ball, then scrambled back up as though the pillow had challenged him.

Heather stood there, mouth slightly open, watching this rare burst of euphoria. “He hasn’t been this excited about a toy in years!” she exclaimed.

Buddy, naturally, took his time arriving at the scene. He sauntered over like a disinterested art critic at a modern gallery, stared at the ball for a full ten seconds, then casually batted it with his paw.

The ball bounced beautifully.

Buddy’s eyes widened. He batted it again, harder.

Sagar looked at Heather. “Apparently I should have bounced the roti.”

Within moments, both dogs were fully invested in what would go down in history as the Great Evening of Bouncy Bliss.

Neet was doing parkour off the furniture. Buddy tried to trap the ball by lying on top of it. It slipped away, leaving him on his side with nothing under him but floor.

Neet stopped beside the escaped ball. Buddy stayed down for a moment, studying it.

“The other side,” Buddy decided.

He got up and tried again from the opposite direction. The ball had an escape route on that side too.

By the end of the night, the living room looked like a minor hurricane had passed through, the ball had gone on a full tour of the house, and both dogs were passed out— Neet clutching the ball between his paws like a priceless artifact, and Buddy snoring with his paw lazily resting on it as if to say, “Yeah, I participated too.”

Sagar sat back with a satisfied sigh, sipping his chai.

Heather smiled and shook her head. “I swear, the psychic connection between you and Neet is downright creepy.”

Sagar simply smirked, leaned back, and whispered under his breath,

“Hey, idiot… your roti’s still the best, isn’t it?”

From the depths of his dreams, Neet let out a soft, knowing sigh.

Buddy tightened his paw on the ball. Sagar could keep the roti channel.
