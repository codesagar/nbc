The Flight to India

9 minute read · 32 illustrations

Heather opened a suitcase on the bed. Buddy fetched his favorite blanket. Neet, the orange whirlwind of a chihuahua with Dorito-shaped ears, began zipping around the house, barking orders as though the trip had been his idea.

Buddy, the calm and timid dachshund-border terrier mix, wasn’t so sure. He poked his head into Heather’s suitcase and nudged his blanket toward the top. If they had to go to India, he wanted one thing within reach that smelled right. His tail wagged nervously as Neet bounced onto the bed, declaring himself "Flight Captain Neet."

"You ready for this, Buddy?"

Buddy pushed the blanket higher. Heather tucked it down. Buddy pushed it higher again. They were making progress in opposite directions.

"I… guess so. As long as there aren’t…, you know, too many loud noises."

Heather packed their essentials: travel crates, toys, and treats. She laughed as Neet attempted to pack himself into the suitcase, standing triumphantly atop a pile of socks.

"Captain travels on top," he announced.

Heather lifted him out with one hand and put a sweater in his place.

Buddy watched the sweater settle. "Promotion filled."

Then Heather started closing the suitcase. Buddy caught the blanket corner in his teeth.

She stopped. He stopped. Neither let go.

"That’s coming too," Heather said gently.

Buddy tugged toward his open travel crate. Coming was one thing. Coming in a box he couldn’t reach was quite another.

Heather looked from the blanket to the crate. "Oh. Your hand luggage."

She spread it inside. Buddy climbed in immediately, turned twice, and lay down. His room was ready. India could come to it.

Neet inspected the result. "You’ve packed yourself."

"Underneath. Less competition."

The day of the flight arrived. At the airport, Neet barked at the luggage conveyor belt. Heather’s suitcase disappeared behind a curtain, and he lunged after it until his leash stopped him.

"They’ve taken the sweater!"

Buddy glanced into his crate. His blanket was still there. For the first time all morning, he felt ahead of the arrangements.

Neet next tried to chase a moving cart. Heather shortened his leash; the cart continued away without accepting his instructions. He gave a challenging yip at the metal detector when it beeped.

It beeped again for somebody else.

Neet stared. "It’s answering everyone."

"Busy," Buddy suggested.

Heather kept them beside her while bags and people shuffled forward. Buddy’s floppy ears twitched with every new sound. Neet had challenged three things already. None had stopped doing its job.

"You’re both going to be fine," Heather reassured them, scratching Buddy behind his ears and giving Neet a gentle pat on his tiny head.

At the gate, Buddy found a promising patch of floor between Heather’s chair and the window. Nobody could approach from behind the glass. He went into his open crate, pulled his blanket into a nest, and settled with his back to the room.

Neet remained on his leash beside Heather. Outside, an enormous plane rolled past.

"That one! I choose that one!"

Buddy closed his eyes. Choosing planes could surely happen without him.

An announcement boomed directly above his crate.

Buddy shot upright, bringing the blanket over his head. He could no longer see the airport. He could hear every bit of it.

"Buddy?" Neet said.

The blanket turned toward his voice.

"Your roof’s fallen in."

Buddy backed out of the fold and gave the ceiling a wounded look. Of all the places in this building, he had chosen the one beneath its mouth.

He nosed the crate’s carrying strap toward Heather. She understood the request, lifted the crate to the other side of her chair, and tucked the blanket flat.

"Better?"

Buddy leaned against her shoe. Considerably. The room was noisy on this side too, but at least his wall could scratch his ears.

When boarding began, Neet planted his paws toward the window. The enormous plane was still outside.

Heather turned toward the gate.

"Wrong direction!" he barked, attempting to steer her by sheer conviction.

Buddy stood beside her ankle. He wanted to stay near the chair. Instead he took a step toward Heather, then another.

Neet looked from him to the window.

"The plane’s out there."

"She’s going this way."

Heather scooped Neet into his crate. "Yes, Captain. We use the door."

He inspected the boarding passage as she carried him through it. "Secret entrance," he told Buddy.

Buddy walked beside her on his leash until she settled him in his own crate. His blanket had made it through the secret entrance. This improved his opinion of aviation.

Once on the plane, Neet poked his head out of his travel crate while Heather arranged their bedding. "So, this is what the big leagues look like," he whispered. "Buddy, this must be the cockpit!"

Buddy sighed from his crate. "It’s just a plane, Neet."

"Not just a plane! A flying fortress! I bet they’ll let me fly it."

Buddy pulled one corner of the blanket over his front paws. "I hope they ask someone first."

Heather secured the crates and settled into her seat. Neet sat upright, ready to supervise.

The engine grew louder. The floor began to tremble.

Neet widened his stance. His ears stayed up a moment longer than the rest of his confidence.

As the plane took off, his little body shook. He made a quiet sound that was nothing like an order.

Buddy’s own paws were tucked hard beneath him. He had been listening for every change in the engine. Now there were too many changes to keep track of, and Neet was looking straight at him.

He lifted his head from his blanket and kept his voice steady. "Hey, it’s okay. Just think of it as… a really big car ride."

"Without the road?" Neet asked.

Buddy swallowed. "Heather’s still here."

Her hand rested against the side of Neet’s crate. Neet leaned toward it.

"You’re right. I’m the Flight Captain. I… I’ve got this."

He lowered himself carefully onto his bedding.

"Flying it lying down?" Buddy asked.

"Better grip."

Buddy lay down too. It did help.

Later, the engine settled into a steady hum. Buddy loosened one paw. Then the other. He began arranging the blanket for a proper nap.

Unfortunately, he remembered the gate. He folded a corner over his ears to prevent another announcement from arriving uninvited.

"Have you disappeared?" Neet whispered.

Buddy pretended not to hear.

The crate beside him rustled.

"Buddy?"

He had finally achieved quiet. All he had to do was keep his head down.

There was another rustle, and a much smaller voice.

"Is it still a car ride?"

Buddy pushed the corner off his face. Neet was crouched on his bedding, watching him through the side of the neighboring crate.

"Still a car ride," Buddy said.

He stretched out facing Neet instead of the wall. Heather bent down to check them, and Buddy nosed his blanket toward her hand. She rubbed his cheek, then tucked Neet’s loose bedding around him too.

Neet put his chin down. Buddy watched until his brother’s eyes closed.

His own nap arrived a little later than planned. It stayed longer than he expected.

By the time the meal carts came around, Neet had regained his swagger. He barked enthusiastically at the flight attendant, convinced she was delivering snacks just for him. Heather apologized as the attendant stifled a laugh, handing her a small pack of pretzels that Neet proudly claimed as his in-flight meal.

"You barked at the cart in the airport," Buddy pointed out.

Neet watched Heather open the packet. "That one didn’t have pretzels."

Heather held the bag out of reach while Neet sat as straight as the crate allowed. At last, someone had discovered the controls.

Heather offered their packed treats first. Buddy ate his and rested his chin on the blanket. Neet stared at the pretzels, continuing to sit so beautifully that Heather had to smile.

She gave him a tiny piece. He crunched it with ceremony.

Then she put the packet on her tray and began eating her own lunch.

Neet remained upright.

Buddy opened one eye. "You can stop."

"She might have seconds."

The tray table folded away. Neet continued sitting.

At last Heather noticed. "Oh, sweetheart. Your shift’s over."

She showed him the empty packet. He checked both sides before lying down.

"Long flight," Buddy murmured.

"Terrible staffing."

As the plane began its descent into India, Neet pressed his nose against the window of his crate, marveling at the view below. "Look at all the colors, Buddy! The trees! The houses! The… wait, is that a cow on the road? This place is awesome!"

Buddy tilted his head, peeking out cautiously. "It does look… interesting."

The engine changed its note. Buddy’s head dropped at once.

Neet stopped looking for the cow.

"Still a car ride," he said.

Buddy looked across. Neet’s paws were braced too, but his chin was down and his eyes were on Buddy.

"You’re flying it," Buddy reminded him.

Neet gave the window a very serious look. "Then we should both hold on."

Heather checked their crates before landing, her touch familiar against each side. Buddy let his chin sink into the blanket. Across from him, the Flight Captain stayed lying down.

When the wheels met the ground, Neet announced, "Found the road!"

Heather laughed, a little breathlessly, and stroked both their heads once the plane had stopped. "Well done, my travelers."

Buddy stretched one paw out of his blanket. Neet stretched a paw toward him from the next crate. Neither could quite reach, but Buddy kept his there.

Outside, a baggage cart rolled past.

Neet’s ears lifted. His chin lifted. Buddy waited.

Then Neet put his head back down.

"Off duty," he said.

Buddy closed his eyes. They had arrived in India, and for the moment, nobody was conquering it.
