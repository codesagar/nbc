Buddy and the Golden Glory

8 minute read · 34 illustrations
In this story
  1. The Crowd Gathers
  2. Sir Buddy Takes a Walk
  3. The Ultimate Humiliation
  4. The Scenic Route

The weekend had arrived, and as was tradition, Heather took Neet and Buddy for their weekly park stroll. But this was no ordinary outing—this was Buddy’s first official public appearance with his golden tooth.

Buddy stopped just inside the park and considered the path.

Usually Neet went first. Neet investigated first, barked first, and then explained why whatever had happened was exactly what he'd intended. Buddy generally arrived during the explanation.

Today Buddy wanted to arrive during the arrival.

He stepped ahead of his brother.

“Excuse me.”

Neet looked up. “What for?”

“Coming through.”

Buddy strutted down the park path with an air of pure confidence. The sunlight gleamed off his shiny tooth, catching the eyes of dogs and humans alike.

Neet, who was already annoyed, trotted beside him, grumbling. “Oh look at me, I have a fancy gold tooth, bow before my greatness—PFFT. Whatever.”

Buddy adjusted his head to catch the sunlight. “I didn’t say anything.”

That was the part Neet found most irritating. He had prepared an argument. Buddy was apparently going to win it by standing there.

Heather paused while Buddy looked around. She had brought them for a walk, and he would get one. If he needed a moment to enjoy being outside first, she could enjoy it with him.

Neet tried to move ahead.

Buddy took one small step into the sun.

“Are we walking or displaying?” Neet asked.

Buddy considered this as though both were respectable park activities.

“Yes.”

The Crowd Gathers

The first group to notice Buddy’s glow-up was a pack of golden retrievers, always known for their friendly but slightly nosy nature.

“Whoa, dude!” one of them barked. “Is that… a gold tooth?”

Buddy flashed his tooth. “Oh, this old thing? Just a little upgrade.”

Another retriever wagged his tail. “That’s so baller.”

A pug waddled over, squinting. “I thought gold teeth were just for pirates.”

Buddy grinned wider, making sure the light hit just right. “I am kinda like a pirate. But instead of sailing the seas, I conquer snack bowls.”

The group erupted in admiration.

Neet, standing awkwardly beside him, scowled. “Oh, come on! You guys are acting like he won an award or something.”

The pug kept looking at Buddy. “Does it make a special noise when you chew?”

The pug waited for a chewing noise. Buddy held his smile. Neither seemed prepared to end the demonstration first.

Neet gasped. “EXCUSE ME?!”

A bulldog edged past Neet for a better look. “Move a little? You’re in his light.”

Neet’s ears flattened. “His light?!”

Buddy held the pose. For once, he did not have to move aside for his brother.

Neet stepped left. The bulldog leaned right. Neet stepped right. The bulldog looked at him.

“Now you're in mine.”

Neet sat down with the careful dignity of a dog who had definitely meant to sit down.

The pug was still waiting.

Buddy finally closed his mouth.

“Is that it?” the pug asked.

“That's the tooth,” said Buddy.

“Yes, but the noise?”

“There isn't a performance schedule.”

Neet stood up immediately. “Thank goodness. We can leave.”

Buddy gave the retrievers one last smile before following Heather. They followed for a few steps too.

Neet looked behind him. He had led any number of patrols. Nobody had ever joined one because his mouth was expensive.

Sir Buddy Takes a Walk

As they continued their walk, more and more dogs, and even some humans, took notice of Buddy’s blinged-out tooth.

A poodle gasped. “Such elegance!”

A husky howled. “This guy’s got status now!”

A dachshund barked. “We should call him Sir Buddy!”

Buddy paused at the title. Heather took another step; he hurried to catch up, looking briefly less knightly.

“Sir Buddy,” he repeated quietly.

Neet groaned. “Oh, this is unbearable.”

Heather patted Neet’s head. “Let Buddy have his turn.”

Neet squinted at her. “You’re enjoying this way too much.”

She smiled down at both dogs, then continued along the path.

Buddy decided that a knight should not keep stopping. A knight should proceed splendidly. He could give everyone a good look and have his walk at the same time.

“This way,” he told the dogs beside him.

Neet stared. That was his line.

Buddy lifted his head toward his admirers and began a magnificent procession.

For three steps it was excellent.

Then the path curved and the sunlight came from the wrong side.

Buddy turned his head farther. The dachshund moved round to keep looking. The pug, unwilling to miss anything now that movement was involved, followed the dachshund.

Neet found himself walking behind three dogs going sideways.

“Path,” he said.

Buddy wasn't listening. He had just worked out that he could recover the shine if he faced a little more toward the people behind him.

He turned his shoulders too.

“Buddy. Path.”

Buddy took a backward step and bumped into Neet.

The procession stopped.

Neet's nose was briefly buried in an extremely distinguished brother.

“Wonderful,” he said. “A knight with no reverse warning.”

Heather stopped beside them. “Forward, Bud.”

She waited while Buddy turned around, then made room for a passing walker. Buddy looked at the space he had occupied. His grand entrance had somehow become a small traffic problem.

The dachshund laughed. The pug sat down, prepared for whatever came next.

Buddy could have pretended the whole thing was intentional. Neet was watching hopefully, ready to ask him exactly which part.

Instead, Buddy looked back at his brother.

“Did you see it, though?”

“From underneath you? No.”

Buddy laughed. The dachshund laughed again. Even Heather was smiling as she set off at an ordinary walking pace.

Buddy followed facing forward. He still had the tooth. Apparently he could keep it without supervising everybody's view of it.

Neet took the open space beside him.

“Next time, I lead.”

“Next time, you stand farther back.”

The Ultimate Humiliation

The final straw came when they ran into a Chihuahua gang—a notorious crew of tiny but fierce dogs who roamed the park like a biker gang on tiny paws.

Their leader, a scruffy tan Chihuahua named Tito, stopped beside Neet.

“Is that Buddy with the gold tooth? Can you introduce us?”

Neet drew himself up. At last. A fellow professional.

Then the question finished reaching him. He had been reduced to arranging appointments.

“He’s busy,” Neet said.

“I’m free,” said Buddy.

Neet looked at him. “You were conducting a procession.”

“Finished.”

“How convenient.”

Buddy turned toward Tito. “This side catches the sun better.”

Tito and the others gathered around Buddy. Neet could no longer see the tooth, only the backs of the dogs admiring it.

He went round the group. One Chihuahua shifted to make room for another. Neet went round again and found himself looking at the same back from a different angle.

“I'm still here,” he announced.

“We know,” Tito said pleasantly. “Thanks for the introduction.”

Buddy caught Neet's eye over Tito's head. Neet looked away before Buddy could thank him too.

This was becoming intolerable. Neet had come to the park with a brother and apparently acquired a celebrity who required assistance.

He turned to Heather.

“I need a gold tooth. Immediately.”

Heather sighed. “Neet, I am not putting you through unnecessary dental surgery just to win an ego battle.”

Neet huffed. “Fine. But I demand another kind of upgrade. A diamond collar. A royal cape. SOMETHING.”

Heather laughed. “Let’s see if you behave first.”

Neet muttered under his breath. “I hate everything.”

Heather crouched beside him for a moment. He pushed his head into her hand, still keeping one eye on Buddy's audience. She rubbed behind his ears until he stopped trying to see around her wrist.

Then she stood, ready to continue their walk. Neet joined her at once.

Buddy noticed them moving.

Tito was asking whether he could turn a little farther. Buddy started to oblige. A familiar gap opened beside Heather where his brother was walking, and Buddy looked past the admiring dogs toward it.

He had wanted a turn in front. He hadn't meant to spend his whole walk standing behind.

“Coming?” Neet called without looking back.

Buddy shut his mouth.

“Where's the gold gone?” asked one of the Chihuahuas.

“With me,” Buddy said. “See you next time.”

He stepped out of the group and trotted after Heather and Neet. Tito called goodbye. Buddy turned his head to answer, remembered the procession, and stopped his paws first.

Neet saw.

“Improvement,” he said.

“Thank you, my attendant.”

Neet accelerated.

The Scenic Route

Buddy trotted ahead, then waited where the path forked. Being admired was pleasant. He still wanted to finish the walk with his brother.

Neet caught up and selected the shady path.

“Scenic route,” he said.

Buddy looked at the solid roof of leaves. “Of course.”

Heather followed them into the shade. The dogs walked beside each other now, with Neet choosing the route and Buddy no longer presenting his face to passing strangers.

For a while Neet enjoyed the absence of sparkle.

Then Buddy glanced at him.

“What?” Neet demanded.

“Nothing.”

Buddy was smiling anyway.

And for once in his life, Neet realized something truly terrifying:

Buddy had won.

And it was unbearable.
