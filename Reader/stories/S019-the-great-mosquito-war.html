The Great Mosquito War

8 minute read · 32 illustrations
In this story
  1. Phase One: The Swarm Arrives
  2. Phase Two: The Counterattack
  3. Phase Three: The Unexpected Setback
  4. Phase Four: The Ultimate Showdown

It all started with a single, high-pitched buzz.

Heather had just settled on the couch with a book, Neet curled up beside her, and Buddy sprawled lazily on the cool tile floor. The night was peaceful—until Neet’s ears twitched.

“Did you hear that?” he barked, jumping to his feet.

Buddy lazily flicked his tail. “It’s probably just the wind.”

Then came the second buzz. And the third.

Heather swatted at the air and groaned. “Oh no. The mosquitoes are back.”

Neet’s eyes widened. “Not again.”

Buddy lowered his chin to the tile. He had just found the exact patch where his belly and the floor agreed about everything. Moving would mean starting negotiations elsewhere.

Neet hopped down from the couch and began pacing between Buddy and Heather.

“We protect the reader,” he declared. “Nobody gets past me.”

Heather turned a page. Neet wheeled around to investigate the sound.

Buddy watched him return to his patrol. “Can you protect her from farther away? You’re walking through my evening.”

Phase One: The Swarm Arrives

The first attack was swift. One moment, the living room was quiet; the next, it was a battlefield. A rogue mosquito dive-bombed Heather’s arm, forcing her to smack herself in a desperate attempt to eliminate the enemy.

Neet leapt into action, barking at the tiny invaders. “You shall not pass!”

The mosquito passed.

Heather held her place in the book with one finger. “That one didn’t listen.”

“I’ll say it closer.”

Buddy, attempting to remain calm, swatted at a mosquito with his paw. “Maybe if we just stay still, they won’t notice us.”

He tucked his paw back beneath him. If this worked, he could save the evening without getting up.

Neet stopped pacing. Buddy was unexpectedly being tactical.

Both dogs waited.

A mosquito landed directly on Buddy’s nose.

Buddy screamed.

Neet stared at him.

“I was staying still,” Buddy said, backing away. “It cheated.”

His cool patch lay abandoned. Neet marched onto it.

“Forward position secured.”

Buddy looked from his nose to his brother. The room now had two problems, and only one of them could be seen properly.

Phase Two: The Counterattack

“Alright, this is war,” Heather declared, grabbing the electric mosquito racket from the side table. She set her book facedown beside it. With a dramatic swing, she zapped one of the invaders, sending it spiraling to the ground in a puff of tiny sparks.

Neet was in awe. “She has lightning powers!”

Buddy watched Heather rather than the fallen mosquito. “Then perhaps we can all sit down.”

Neet turned to him. “We need a perimeter.”

“We have walls.”

“A moving perimeter.”

Buddy considered that. He considered his tile. Then he walked to the couch, took the hanging blanket corner in his teeth, and dragged it down.

Neet approved. “Equipment!”

Buddy pushed his nose beneath a fold, bunching part of the blanket into a long, untidy lump. He left it on his old resting patch and stepped away.

“What’s that?” Neet asked.

“Me.”

Neet inspected the lump.

“Mosquitoes can bother that,” Buddy explained. “I’ll be over here.”

Heather glanced down. “Very handsome, Buddy. Bit flat.”

The real Buddy settled nearer the couch. His substitute had no nose to land on. He felt this was a considerable improvement.

Determined to contribute, Neet launched himself into battle, snapping at the mosquitoes mid-air. He spun, twisted, and lunged, yapping furiously. He managed to bite the air about a hundred times—and absolutely nothing else.

On his next circuit, he bounded over the blanket lump.

Buddy sat up. “That’s supposed to look occupied.”

Neet bounded over it again.

“Stop jumping over me.”

“You’re over there.”

“You’re ruining the impression.”

Buddy pulled at the loose corner to restore his decoy. Neet planted a paw on the other end as he pivoted. The blanket stretched, flattened, and slid off the cool patch.

Neet stepped free and stared at the wreckage.

“Your position collapsed.”

“You stood on it!”

Heather lowered the racket while the two dogs examined their defeat by a blanket. A mosquito sailed past her shoulder.

Buddy looked at the empty lump. It had attracted one general and no mosquitoes. He abandoned the experiment.

He dragged one corner toward the couch, crawled beneath it, then backed out to collect the paw he had left sticking into enemy territory.

Heather turned to him. “Really, Buddy?”

A muffled voice from beneath the blanket responded. “You handle this. I’m too pretty to be bitten.”

Neet approached the opening.

“Come out. I need a second patrol.”

Buddy pulled the corner shut.

The perimeter remained small and stationary.

Phase Three: The Unexpected Setback

Just as Heather was about to deliver another electrifying blow, the racket fizzled out.

“Battery’s dead,” she whispered, horror-struck.

She pressed the button again. Both dogs waited for the lightning.

The little plastic click was not reassuring.

Neet gasped. “We’re doomed.”

Buddy poked his head out. “Have we tried being doomed quietly?”

Heather turned toward the side table to put the racket on charge. Her foot stopped before the blanket spread across her path.

“Buddy. I need that bit of floor.”

Buddy looked at the table, then at the blanket. His defense had occupied the route to the lightning.

Neet hurried over. “Evacuate! Move everything!”

Buddy took one edge in his teeth. Neet took another and pulled in the opposite direction.

Heather stood still, holding the racket, while the blanket became very straight between them.

Buddy let go.

Neet sat down abruptly with his end.

“Excellent,” Buddy said. “Hold that.”

He went around to Neet’s side and dragged the free folds toward the couch instead. Neet’s corner followed. A strip of tile opened between Heather and the table.

“There we are. Thank you,” Heather said, passing them.

Buddy watched her connect the racket to its charger, its cable running from the table to the wall. Then he put his chin on the newly cleared tile.

For one excellent moment, helpfulness and coolness were the same thing.

A mosquito landed on Heather’s forehead. She swatted. She missed. The mosquito, now emboldened, buzzed in victory.

“That’s it,” Heather growled. “Plan B.”

She grabbed a bottle of mosquito repellent. Neet rose at once.

“New weapon. I’ll cover you.”

Buddy lifted his chin. “She can reach the whole room from there.”

Buddy backed under his blanket. Neet moved closer anyway. He sprang up just as Heather pressed the nozzle and caught the spray in his face.

“AHHH! I’M UNDER ATTACK!” Neet howled, shaking his head wildly and sprinting in frantic circles.

Heather put down the bottle at once.

“Neet, come here.”

He completed another circle before finding her waiting hand. She crouched beside him and wiped his face with a damp cloth, her other hand resting gently along his back.

The racket stayed on the table. The war could wait.

Buddy pushed his blanket aside. “There’s room under here,” he offered.

Neet looked at the opening, then at Heather. She wiped his muzzle once more.

He shook his head. He still had a war to finish.

Buddy left the corner open anyway.

Phase Four: The Ultimate Showdown

With the repellent now properly aimed at the mosquitoes and not Neet, Heather managed to push them back. But one mosquito, larger and bolder than the rest, refused to retreat.

It hovered menacingly in the air, staring Heather down.

“This one’s the leader,” Neet whispered. “We have to take it down.”

Heather set the bottle aside again. While the racket charged, the mosquito circled beneath the light as though inspecting the room for somewhere especially annoying to land.

Neet crouched on the cleared tile. Buddy watched from the blanket opening.

“Don’t jump in front of her again,” Buddy said.

“That was reconnaissance.”

“You found the spray.”

Neet shifted to one side. “I’ll attack from here.”

Heather checked the racket after a pause, disconnected the charger, and returned beneath the light. Buddy drew the blanket around himself again, leaving the corner open for Neet.

The mosquito descended a little.

Heather raised the racket.

Neet lowered his head.

Buddy remembered his exposed paw and pulled it in.

The mosquito dove straight at Heather. She swung. Missed.

Neet leapt. Missed.

Buddy sneezed under his blanket.

And then, in an act of sheer luck, the mosquito veered too close to the ceiling fan. With one dramatic whirrrrr, it was sucked into the abyss and vanished forever.

Silence.

Then Neet, panting, collapsed onto the floor. “We did it.”

Buddy peeked out. “Did we win?”

Heather wiped sweat from her forehead. “I don’t know if we won, but we survived.”

She laid the racket on the table and stood looking at the blanket, the dogs, and the book that was still on the same page.

Then she laughed. Buddy let his chin drop. That sounded much more like the evening he had wanted.

Neet stood, his face still damp from Heather’s cloth. “We defended our home.” He looked up at the fan. “With air support.”

Buddy yawned. “You’re really milking this, huh?”

He looked up at the fan, then shuffled his blanket directly underneath it. This time he left no decoy. He would occupy his own position.

Neet followed him. “I’m inspecting our defenses.”

“From in here?”

“Closely.”

Buddy lifted the corner. This time, Neet went in.

Buddy shifted enough to give him space on the tile. The blanket settled over both of them. No patrol passed through anybody’s evening.

Heather shook her head and collapsed onto the couch. “Next time, we’re getting a mosquito net.”

She picked up her book, found her place, and began the page again.

And so, the Great Mosquito War came to an end… at least until the next summer night.
