Buddy’s Big Dental Adventure (Part 1)

7 minute read · 28 illustrations

It all started with a single toothache.

Buddy had been feeling a little off lately—chewing slower, avoiding his favorite crunchy treats, and giving Heather a concerned look whenever he tried to gnaw on his chew toy.

He wanted his ordinary morning back. Pick up toy. Enjoy toy. Put toy somewhere Neet couldn't immediately find it. There had never been anything complicated about this arrangement before.

Now he picked it up, put it down, and stared at it.

Neet appeared beside him. “Are you finished with that?”

“No.”

“You've put it down.”

“I'm keeping it.”

Neet considered the uneaten treat too. Buddy moved a paw beside it. It was a miserable little collection to defend: two things he wanted and couldn't enjoy.

Heather crouched beside him. “What's the matter, Bud?”

Buddy tipped his face toward her. Neet attempted an inspection, sticking his own face far too close.

“OH WOW, IT’S GROSS IN HERE!”

Buddy closed his mouth.

“Inspection complete,” Neet announced.

“Your part is,” Buddy said.

He left the toy where it was and took his sore jaw to Heather. She put down his uneaten treat and called the vet. While she talked, Buddy leaned against her knee. She rubbed his head without making him demonstrate the problem again.

Neet looked at the abandoned toy.

Buddy looked at Neet.

Neet sat somewhere else.

The Waiting Room Chronicles

As they arrived at the vet’s office, Buddy let out a long sigh. “This is going to be a long day.”

Buddy tucked himself beside Heather’s knees. If he kept very still, perhaps they could wait without anything happening.

Neet, however, was thrilled. “So many smells! So many pets! We should make friends!”

“Quiet friends,” Buddy said.

“Those are harder to find.”

Heather checked them in while Buddy found a seat next to a grumpy-looking tabby cat in a carrier. The cat squinted at him. “What’s your deal?”

Buddy sighed. “Dental surgery.”

The cat shuddered. “Been there. Lost a tooth once. My human called me ‘Gappy’ for six months.”

Buddy shifted to the far end of the seat. He had wanted reassurance. He now had a possible nickname.

“Did you try not answering?” he asked.

“Of course.”

“Did that work?”

“They thought I was being a cat.”

Buddy looked toward Heather. She smiled when she saw him watching and came to sit beside him. He pressed close to her, relieved to be with somebody who already knew his name.

Neet, already nose-deep in someone else’s business, trotted over to a gigantic Great Dane. “What’s wrong with you?”

The Great Dane blinked slowly. “I swallowed a sock.”

Neet gasped. “A WHOLE SOCK?! You’re a legend!”

The Dane sighed. “It was not on purpose.”

Neet looked him over with new respect. Buddy could practically see a second, much less sensible reason to visit the vet forming in his brother's head.

“Was it at least a clean—”

“Neet,” Buddy said. “Let him have a private sock.”

The Dane gave Buddy a grateful look.

Neet came back, still impressed. “He doesn't even want to discuss it.”

“Yes,” Buddy said. “That was the thing he wanted.”

Neet sat beside him. For a moment, Buddy had both Heather and his brother close, and nobody asking to see inside his mouth. He let his shoulders settle.

“Was there another sock?” Neet whispered.

Buddy closed his eyes.

Heather stroked his back. “Let him rest a minute, Neet.”

Neet looked from her to Buddy, then folded his paws with tremendous care. Buddy opened one eye. His brother was sitting so attentively that several strangers had begun to look at him.

“Less,” Buddy whispered.

Neet lowered his chin. His ears remained on duty.

Buddy tried to match him. Paws still. Mouth shut. No further questions. Perhaps they could become two ordinary dogs who had merely come in for somewhere to sit.

Then the vet tech called Buddy's name.

Neet's head sprang up. Buddy stayed perfectly still.

She smiled toward Heather. “Buddy?”

Heather touched his shoulder. “That's you, sweetheart.”

He looked at her, then at the open doorway. Keeping still had achieved exactly what it always achieved at home: Heather knew where to find him.

Buddy stood.

At least nobody had called him Gappy.

The Betrayal (Aka, Heather Leaves)

Buddy gulped as Heather patted his head. “Be a good boy, Bud. I’ll be back soon.”

Soon sounded promising. He waited for her to take the first step with him.

Instead, he watched in horror as she walked out the door.

“SHE LEFT ME!” Buddy yelped. “I’VE BEEN ABANDONED!”

Heather turned at the sound and met his eyes. “I'll be back, Buddy.” She stayed long enough for him to see her smile, then went on.

Buddy watched the door close. He knew back. He liked back. He would have preferred it to happen already.

Neet, still standing in the lobby, waved dramatically. “DON’T WORRY! I’LL INHERIT YOUR CHEW TOYS!”

Buddy stopped protesting long enough to turn around. “The blue one is still mine.”

Neet lowered his waving paw and sat down.

“And the others,” Buddy added.

Neet's mouth opened, then closed.

There. Buddy had not solved the doorway problem, but he had prevented a considerable problem at home.

The vet tech crouched a little and waited. Buddy looked toward the outer door once more. Then he looked at Neet, who was still sitting with his waving paw tucked away.

“Stay with Heather,” Buddy said.

Neet nodded. No grand announcement followed it.

That settled, Buddy took one reluctant step toward the vet tech. She waited for him to look back one last time, then guided him inside. “All right, big guy. Let’s get that tooth sorted.”

He went with her. He still wanted his ordinary morning back, even if getting there had turned into a very unusual day.

The Land of Anesthesia and Strange Conversations

Buddy was placed on the exam table while the vet checked his chart.

He looked at the doorway. No Heather. No Neet. Nobody to interrupt an explanation with a question about socks.

For the first time all morning, Buddy wished Neet would interrupt one.

“Alright, looks like we’ve got one tooth causing trouble. We’ll remove it and get you feeling better in no time.”

Buddy swallowed. “Will I survive?”

The vet rested a hand on his shoulder. “We’ll look after you.”

Buddy leaned against the hand. It wasn’t Heather’s, but it would do for now. He looked up at the vet, who stayed beside him. He didn't have to keep still enough to be forgotten. He could be here and be looked after.

Then came the anesthesia.

As the sedative kicked in, the room around Buddy started to shift. The lights got brighter. The walls stretched. And suddenly…

The vet dogs started talking.

A Labrador in a nurse’s cap leaned in. “How you feelin’, champ?”

Buddy squinted. “Like I’m floating… in a gravy boat.”

A scruffy terrier from the corner barked, “OH BOY, IT’S HITTIN’ HIM!”

Buddy looked down. He was quite certain he had brought four paws. They seemed to be arriving at his attention one at a time.

“Do we all fit?” he asked.

The Labrador tipped her head.

“Me,” Buddy explained, as though this made the question easier. “All of me.”

He tried to look toward the doorway. It drifted away before he could decide which side Heather would come through.

Buddy waved his paw lazily. “Wait… am I still a dog?”

The Labrador nodded. “Mhm. But right now? You’re a cool dog.”

Buddy grinned dopily. “I am cool.”

Cool sounded much better than Gappy. He would have to tell the cat.

He pictured returning to the waiting room and saying it very casually. No announcement. Just Buddy, who was cool, passing through on his way to Heather.

“Neet doesn't know,” he murmured.

The terrier howled. “SOMEONE GET THIS GUY A GOLD TOOTH!”

Buddy tried to tell them that he had only come about the sore tooth. He also meant to ask whether Heather would know where to collect a gravy boat.

There were suddenly rather a lot of things to explain, and all of them were floating.

“Not too far,” he murmured.

The gravy boat was already carrying him away.
