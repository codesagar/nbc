Winter Woes and the Battle of the Backyard

8 minute read · 30 illustrations
In this story
  1. The Great Outdoor Stand-Off
  2. Buddy Improves the Backyard
  3. The Reluctant Poop
  4. The Indoor Victory Lap

Winter had arrived, and with it came icy winds, frigid mornings, and Neet’s absolute refusal to cooperate.

Heather groaned as her alarm buzzed at 6:30 AM. She bundled herself up in multiple layers before trudging towards the door where Buddy and Neet waited—one eager, the other on the verge of staging a full-scale protest.

“I am not going out there,” Neet declared, dramatically flopping onto the floor.

Buddy rolled his eyes. “Neet, it’s just for a few minutes. We do this every morning.”

Heather sighed, pulling on her gloves. “Neet, unless you’ve learned how to use the toilet indoors, you have to go outside.”

Neet scoffed. “I can hold it until spring.”

Buddy put his nose against the door. His plan was to be back by the heater before the warm spot forgot him.

“Spring can come. I’m going now.”

Heather reached for the handle. Neet scrambled upright.

“Wait. She’s putting things on.”

Buddy looked at Heather’s boots.

“Yes.”

“And things over the things.”

Heather tucked her scarf into her coat. Neet inspected his own bare chest, then looked up at her.

“I seem to be missing several things.”

She crouched and rubbed his ears. “We’ll be quick, sweetheart.”

Neet leaned into the rub. Perhaps she had forgotten the door. Buddy nudged the handle side with his nose, and Heather stood.

Neet stared at him. “You could have let that develop.”

The Great Outdoor Stand-Off

Heather coaxed Neet over the threshold. The icy wind did not help her case.

Neet yelped. “THIS IS INHUMANE!”

Buddy, already trotting toward the designated bathroom spot, sighed. “Just get it over with, Neet.”

Neet, still clinging to the porch, narrowed his eyes. “You go first. I need to assess the dangers.”

Buddy picked his spot and got down to business. Heather watched as he finished, shook the snow off his paws, and trotted back victoriously.

“See? Not so bad,” Buddy said. “Now your turn.”

He stood beside the door, ready for it to open. It didn’t. He looked at Heather, then at Neet, then at the door again.

Neet peered at the snow-covered ground like it was lava. “I’ll sink! The snow will eat me!”

Heather pinched the bridge of her nose. “Neet, you weigh five pounds. You’re not going to sink.”

Neet’s ears flattened. “I can’t feel my tail already. This is the end for me.”

Buddy nudged Neet toward the patch he had already tested. “Here. Less snow.” He wanted them both indoors before his paws went cold again. He had completed his morning and somehow been assigned another one.

Neet finally relented, stepping delicately into the snow. His paws touched the frost, and he froze, metaphorically and literally. His face contorted in absolute betrayal. He lifted one front paw, put it down to lift the other, then looked offended by the available number of legs.

“I HATE THIS! I HATE EVERYTHING!” he howled dramatically.

Heather hid a smile in her scarf. “Even breakfast?”

Neet stopped howling.

“Most things.”

Buddy stepped back into his own tracks. He could still see the grass where he had been. All Neet needed to do was cross a few feet of yard.

“Put your paws where mine were.”

Neet examined the first print, lowered a paw into it, and waited.

“Well?” Buddy asked.

“Checking.”

“It’s a hole.”

“That’s what worries me.”

He tried the next print. This time both front paws reached the ground. His back paws remained on the bottom step, leaving him stretched between the porch and his objections.

Heather waited beside the steps. “You’ve nearly got it.”

Neet glanced behind him. The porch was still available. He brought all four paws back onto it.

Buddy looked at the abandoned prints. His brilliant shortcut had become a series of separate appointments.

Buddy Improves the Backyard

Buddy returned to Neet and tried again.

“You don’t have to use every print. Just follow me.”

He walked a shorter line to the grass and looked back. Neet followed, placing each paw so carefully that Buddy could practically feel the heater cooling in his absence.

Then Neet sniffed the patch.

“Too much snow.”

Buddy could see grass. He could smell grass. He had personally used this grass. Nevertheless, he scraped aside the loose snow with a front paw.

“There.”

Neet leaned down for another inspection just as Buddy made one final sweep.

A small spray landed across Neet’s nose.

Both dogs stopped.

Heather pressed her lips together. Buddy wished she would laugh and get it over with.

Neet blinked. “You have thrown winter at me.”

“I was moving it.”

“Onto me.”

Buddy looked at the cleared ground. The patch was bigger. So was the problem.

Heather brushed the snow from Neet’s muzzle with her glove. “He was trying to help. There you go.”

Neet sniffed her glove. Warm hand underneath. He pushed his cheek against it before she straightened.

Buddy waited for him to use the newly improved patch.

Instead, Neet inspected the little ridge of snow beside it.

“What’s that?”

“The snow that was here.”

“So it’s waiting.”

Buddy shut his eyes for a moment. The whole yard was waiting. It had considerably more time than he did.

Heather stepped around the ridge and brushed a little more snow aside with her boot. Neet approached the place she had cleared.

Buddy opened his eyes.

“I just did that.”

“Hers is different,” Neet said.

“How?”

Neet lowered his nose. “I’m finding out.”

Buddy resisted the urge to improve anything else.

The Reluctant Poop

After what felt like an eternity, Neet finally found the perfect spot.

It was beside the patch Buddy had offered in the first place.

Heather, already shivering, encouraged him. “Come on, Neet, just finish up so we can go inside.”

Neet turned in seventeen circles. Buddy followed the first two, thinking they were looking for somewhere better. On the third he stopped.

“Same spot,” he said as Neet passed him again.

Neet sniffed. Buddy moved out of his way. Neet circled into the space he had vacated.

“Still the same spot.”

Heather pulled her sleeves over her gloves. Buddy tucked himself behind her boots to borrow the windbreak.

Neet noticed him as he came around again.

“What are you doing?”

“Standing where it’s less windy.”

Neet abandoned his circle and squeezed in beside him.

Buddy moved over. He had invited no one, but Neet’s shoulder was warm.

For a moment, the two of them stood together behind Heather’s legs while the wind ruffled Buddy’s beard.

Then Neet began turning.

His nose bumped Buddy’s side.

Buddy stepped away. Neet’s next turn brought him against Heather’s boot. He backed up, met her other boot, and looked offended.

“There isn’t enough room.”

“There is in the yard,” Buddy said.

Heather looked down at the shuffle behind her. “I’m not a bathroom, you two.”

She laughed into her scarf and stepped aside. Neet watched his shelter walk away.

Buddy returned to the spot behind her new position. This time he stayed close to one boot. Neet looked at the available space, then reluctantly returned to the grass.

He completed his seventeenth circle. Buddy had no intention of helping with an eighteenth.

Neet checked the wind and inspected the sky for predator drones.

Nothing moved overhead. He checked again, more suspiciously.

Heather bent slightly towards him. “Yes. Right there. You can do it.”

Buddy leaned around her boot. Finally.

A gust of wind blew directly in Neet’s face.

Neet yelped and sprinted back to the porch. “NOPE! Nature is against me! I’m canceling this mission!”

Heather groaned. “NEET!”

Buddy emerged from behind her boots. All that inspection, and Neet had chosen the porch.

He looked at the cleared patches. He had offered a route, improved the ground, and shared his windbreak. The only dog who had finished remained the one who had started before all the improvements.

This time Buddy went straight to the door.

“You know this means you’ll have to go back outside later, right?”

Neet scoffed. “I will train my body to absorb waste before I step into that hellscape again.”

Heather shook her head. “Fine. But if you have an emergency inside, you’re explaining it to the carpet cleaner.”

Neet gulped. “Noted.”

She bent to rub a little snow from Buddy’s paw, then opened the door. He went through before Neet could inspect the threshold.

The Indoor Victory Lap

The moment they stepped back inside, Neet immediately came back to life.

“Oh wow, home is so nice. I love our house. So warm. So cozy.” He stretched dramatically before diving into his blanket cocoon.

Buddy went straight to the heater and lowered himself into the warm spot. He put both front paws in it before Neet could propose sharing.

“You’re so dramatic,” he muttered.

Heather collapsed onto the couch. “Tomorrow, we’re starting earlier. No negotiations.”

Neet peeked out from his blanket fortress. “We’ll see.”

Heather shook her head, smiling now that her fingers were warming. She tucked the blanket’s loose corner beside him. Neet pressed his nose into it, perfectly willing to accept this improvement.

Buddy stretched his paws toward the heater. Neet tucked his nose beneath the blanket.

“If I need to go later—” Neet began.

“You know the route,” said Buddy, and closed his eyes.
