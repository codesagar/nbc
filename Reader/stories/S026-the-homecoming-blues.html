The Homecoming Blues

9 minute read · 36 illustrations

  2. The Auto Rickshaw Withdrawal
  3. The Bollywood Dance-Off
  4. The Chai Conundrum
  5. The Melodramatic Monsoon

After their whirlwind adventures abroad, Heather, Neet, and Buddy finally settled back into their cozy suburban home. The familiar sights and sounds of the U.S. surrounded them, but something felt... off.

Neet sprawled dramatically across the couch, sighing loudly. "It's so... quiet here."

Buddy glanced up from his spot on the rug. "Isn't that a good thing?"

"No honking, no street vendors, no spontaneous cow parades," Neet continued, ignoring Buddy. "I miss the chaos!"

Heather chuckled as she unpacked their suitcases. "I never thought I'd hear you say that."

Buddy closed his eyes. The refrigerator hummed. A bird chirped.

Neet sighed again, louder.

"The quiet's putting up a fight," Buddy said.

He had wanted this rug all the way home. Now he was on it, and Neet was making it sound like a disappointing layover. Buddy tucked his nose beneath a paw. There was nothing wrong with their house. Tomorrow, perhaps, they could take their old walk to the park and prove it.

There was a tree there Buddy particularly wanted to inspect. They had been away. Things might have happened.

The Great Spice Crisis

One evening, Heather decided to cook dinner. She reached into the spice cabinet and frowned. "Where's the garam masala?"

Neet perked up. "Probably still in India, where the real flavor is."

Buddy nodded. "Remember the street food? The samosas, the chaat..."

Neet's eyes glazed over. "The pani puri... Oh, the glorious pani puri..."

For a moment Buddy could almost hear the busy street around them. Then the refrigerator started humming again.

Heather sighed. "I guess we'll have to make do with what we have."

Neet gasped. "Make do? With these bland spices? This is culinary blasphemy!"

Buddy sniffed the open cabinet. "We could start by finding the salt and pepper." He missed the food too, but dinner still had to happen here.

Heather set the two little shakers on the floor beside the cabinet while she searched the back shelf. Buddy nosed the salt toward Neet, offering what help he could. Neet stared at the pair.

"That's the entire rescue party?"

"They came together," Buddy said.

Neet lowered his nose to the pepper shaker, considered it, and sneezed. Buddy backed up before the inspection could include him.

Heather found the cumin behind the cinnamon. Both dogs immediately abandoned the salt.

She opened the jar and let them sniff from a little distance. Neet's ears lifted. Buddy leaned forward until Heather gently moved it away.

"Aha. So this kitchen has survived after all."

Neet followed her to the stove. When the familiar smell began rising from dinner, he sat down to supervise instead of returning to the couch.

Buddy returned the neglected salt toward the cabinet with his nose. It had done nothing wrong. He understood the feeling.

The Auto Rickshaw Withdrawal

A few days later, Heather suggested a walk to the park. Buddy was at the door before she had finished the sentence.

Heather clipped on both leashes. At last: the familiar route, the familiar tree, and no luggage.

As they strolled through the quiet neighborhood, Neet looked around wistfully.

"Remember the auto rickshaws? The thrill of weaving through traffic, the wind in my fur..."

Buddy raised an eyebrow. "You mean the terror of near-death experiences?"

Neet sighed. "It was exhilarating."

Heather laughed. "Well, here we have... minivans."

Neet groaned. "It's just not the same."

A minivan stopped at the crossing. The driver waved them through. Buddy hurried gratefully across; Neet paused to study this failure of nerve.

"It's letting us win."

Heather gave his leash a gentle tug. "Accept graciously."

At the park entrance, Buddy caught the smell of his tree. Then a lawn mower started farther along the path.

He had an idea. Neet wanted noise. There was noise. They could enjoy it briefly, then return to the important business of the tree.

Buddy took the path toward the sound. Heather followed, and Neet hurried beside him.

"Finally. An engine with something to say."

They stopped well short of the man mowing the grass. The machine rumbled, turned, and came back along the next strip. Neet waited for the honk. There wasn't one.

"It's going up and down."

"Very reliably," Buddy said.

The mower turned again. Neet's ears lowered.

"Even the engines have routines."

Buddy looked back down the path. His tree was out of sight now. He had traded something he actually wanted for a performance Neet didn't even like.

When Heather turned them toward home, Buddy followed reluctantly. Neet walked beside him without another complaint, which somehow felt worse.

The Bollywood Dance-Off

One night, Heather found Neet in front of the TV, attempting to dance.

"What are you doing?" she asked.

"Trying to recreate the magic of Bollywood," Neet replied, striking a dramatic pose.

Buddy watched him miss the same step twice. "Is that the bit the actor did, or your contribution?"

Neet huffed. "You just don't appreciate art."

Heather smiled. "Maybe we can find a local dance class?"

Neet's eyes lit up. "Do you think they have Bollywood dance classes here?"

Buddy got up and tried one careful step beside him. Neet stopped dancing to correct it, immediately less homesick.

Buddy put the paw down, then lifted the other one.

"No, the first one."

"It was wrong a second ago."

The dancers on the screen turned. Neet turned after them. Buddy, still waiting for permission to put his paw down, turned late and bumped his shoulder into Neet's.

Both stopped. On the TV, an entire crowd continued without them.

Heather sat down, smiling. "Shall we try that again?"

She went back to the beginning of the song. Buddy decided to watch the dancers himself this time. One step, pause, turn. He managed the turn without hitting anybody and looked up, delighted.

Neet had turned the other way.

Buddy waited. Neet looked from him to the screen, then quietly turned back.

"I was checking the rear."

"Secure?"

"Completely."

Heather clapped along. The dogs tried again, close enough to share the music and far enough apart to retain their shoulders.

For the first time all week, nobody compared the living room unfavorably with another continent.

When the song ended, Neet stayed standing, listening for the next one.

The Chai Conundrum

Heather decided to surprise the boys with some homemade chai. She set a cup before each of them.

Neet took a sip and immediately spat it out. "What is this?"

"Chai," Heather said, taken aback.

"This is not chai," Neet declared. "This is an insult to chai."

Buddy took a cautious sip. "It's... different."

Neet shook his head. "We need to find a proper chaiwala. Immediately."

Heather laughed. "I'll see what I can do."

Buddy pushed his cup gently away, determined not to offend her. It bumped Neet's.

"Are you giving me yours?"

"I was hoping we could share a complaint."

Neet tried to push it back. His paw caught the rim instead. The cup tipped, and chai spread across the floor between them.

Heather collected both cups. "Wonderful. Less washing up."

Buddy stepped back from the puddle. He had meant to be polite. Now Heather had a floor to clean as well as two disappointed customers.

She set the cups on the table, brought a cloth from the kitchen, and knelt to wipe the spill. Neet sniffed the air again.

"Still the same," Buddy told him.

"I'm checking."

Heather sat back on her heels. "You know, I miss sitting there with a cup too."

Neet sat beside her. For once, he had no correction to make.

Buddy watched Heather fold the damp cloth. He remembered the chair she had sat in, the street beyond it, the way they had all stopped being busy for a while. He had been trying to give Neet the noise back. Maybe that wasn't the part Neet wanted most.

Heather finished wiping and held out her free hand. Buddy went to it, then Neet. She scratched one head and then the other.

"We'll keep looking."

They stayed beside her while she sat down with the remaining chai.

The Melodramatic Monsoon

One afternoon, it started to rain. Neet pressed his nose against the window, watching the drizzle.

"It's not the same," he murmured.

Buddy looked up. "What isn't?"

"The monsoons," Neet said. "In India, the rain was... alive. Here, it's just wet."

Heather patted his head. "Maybe we can visit during monsoon season next year."

Neet sighed. "It's not just the rain. It's the spirit of it all."

Buddy almost offered to find him a louder puddle. Instead, he nudged him.

"Come on. We can miss it and still go for a walk."

Neet managed a small smile. "I suppose you're right."

Buddy waited beside the door while Heather found the leashes. Neet gave the rain one more doubtful look, then joined him. Heather clipped them on. It was still only wet. But there might be something worth smelling in it.

Outside, Buddy plunged his nose into the damp grass. Neet tried the next patch.

"Anything?" Buddy asked.

Neet sniffed again, thoroughly. "Too early to condemn it."

Heather let them take their time.

At the park entrance, Buddy took the path toward his tree. No diversion this time. He stopped beside the trunk and breathed in an astonishing quantity of news.

Neet waited a few steps away.

"You coming?"

Buddy kept his nose down. "In a minute. I missed this."

Neet came back. He sniffed the grass beside Buddy, then moved around the tree until Heather followed them, giving both leashes room.

Something had been here. Something small, busy, and completely unconcerned with international travel.

Neet sniffed higher. A drop fell from a leaf onto his nose.

He jumped back. Buddy looked up, then received a drop on his own nose.

Heather laughed. The dogs stared accusingly into the branches.

"Alive enough?" Buddy asked.

Neet wiped his nose against his foreleg. "It's making an effort."

They stayed until Buddy had finished his inspection. On the way home, Neet stopped at a patch they had passed without noticing before. Buddy waited for him too.

They still missed India. The cumin hadn't replaced the street food, and the lawn mower was never going to become a rickshaw. But when Heather opened their own front door, both dogs hurried through it beside her. She unclipped their leashes.

Buddy reached his rug first. Neet paused, considered the couch, and settled on the rug next to him.

The refrigerator hummed.

This time, neither dog objected.
