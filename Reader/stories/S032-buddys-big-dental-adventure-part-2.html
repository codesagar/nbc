Buddy’s Big Dental Adventure (Part 2)

7 minute read · 27 illustrations

Buddy slowly blinked awake. The world felt soft, warm, and incredibly weird.

His mouth was numb. His tongue felt three times its size. He tried to move but realized his legs were made of spaghetti.

“Whoa…” he murmured. “I think I’m levitating.”

Across the room, a sleepy basset hound snorted. “Dude, you are very much on the ground.”

Buddy considered the blanket underneath him. The ground was making a convincing effort to look like a blanket. That didn't settle anything.

He turned his head—or at least, he thought he did. Everything wobbled. “I just had… surgery? Am I… a wizard now?”

The Labrador nurse from earlier trotted over, chuckling. To Buddy, the strange conversations were continuing as though the gravy boat had merely stopped somewhere.

“Not quite, champ. But you do have a gold tooth now.”

Buddy gasped. “I’M RICH?!”

A scruffy terrier from the far end of the room howled with laughter. “WE GOT A FANCY ONE, BOYS!”

Buddy, still processing his new golden status, turned to a pug lying next to him. “How do I look? Be honest.”

The pug squinted. “You look… important. Like you should be in a rap video.”

Buddy grinned drowsily. “I knew it.”

He tried to sit a little more importantly. His chin sank back onto the blanket.

“A lying-down sort of important,” the basset said.

Buddy decided that was the best sort.

“Tell Heather,” he said to the pug.

“Tell her what?”

Buddy paused. There had been a very good reason for this request. He had just heard it.

“That I'm here.”

The pug looked around. “I don't think she asked me.”

Buddy hadn't considered that. He put his chin down again and watched the doorway instead. When Heather came, he would go home with her. Rich or not, that was the part he wanted most.

The Pickup and the Post-Surgery Struggle

Eventually, Heather arrived to pick up Buddy, who was still wobbling like a marshmallow on stilts.

“Hey, Bud,” she cooed, kneeling down. “How’re you feeling?”

Buddy, still loopy, tilted his head. “Heather… I have become elite.”

Heather blinked. “Oh boy.”

He leaned into her hand. She stayed beside him, smiling as he took his time remembering how standing was supposed to work.

The vet tech chuckled. “He’s feeling good. He might be a little goofy for the next few hours.”

Buddy wanted to leave gracefully. Heather had come back exactly as promised, and he intended to meet her with the dignity of a dog whose day had gone very well.

He stepped forward. The rest of him went slightly sideways.

Heather steadied him without fuss. “There you are.”

Buddy looked up. She wasn't laughing at the entrance he had meant to make. She was glad to have him against her leg. He decided they could leave like this.

Neet, who had been waiting in the car, watched Buddy wobble toward him.

“Why are you walking sideways?”

Buddy paused beside Neet. “Neet… I can see sound.”

Neet tilted his head. “Can you hear me, then?”

Buddy leaned against Heather's leg instead of answering.

Heather steadied Buddy. “Slowly, Bud.”

Buddy planted one paw, considered it, and put the next beside it. Heather waited. Neet opened his mouth to offer instructions.

“One of you at a time,” Buddy told the two Neets.

The single Neet closed his mouth.

This made the situation considerably better. Buddy looked toward the car, then back at his brother.

“Stay there.”

Neet froze. Buddy took another careful step.

“Here?” Neet asked.

“Good. You're both doing very well.”

Heather reached the car with Buddy beside her. Neet followed only when she turned toward him. He had received praise for standing still and was reluctant to jeopardize it.

The Bathroom Fiasco

Once they got home, Heather took Buddy and Neet outside for their usual bathroom break.

Buddy recognized the familiar route. At last, something he had done before. He moved ahead, determined to complete one ordinary task without anybody telling him where to put his paws.

He walked directly into a bush.

Neet peered into the leaves. “Buddy? Wrong entrance.”

Buddy stopped. He had expected more open space and significantly fewer twigs.

“Has this moved?”

“No.”

That seemed unlikely, but Buddy wasn't in a position to inspect the rest of the garden. He groggily backed out of the bush. A leaf rode out on his eyebrow.

“Where… where’s the ground?”

Neet wheezed. “Buddy, you’re standing on it.”

Heather brushed the leaf away. “Come this way, sweetheart.”

Buddy followed her hand. He could accept a little guidance. He hadn't intended to spend the afternoon inside a plant.

After regaining some composure, he finally got into position to relieve himself—except nothing happened.

He blinked. “Why… why is it not working?”

Neet began to laugh, then stopped when Buddy looked back at him.

Buddy looked panicked. “What if I never pee again?! This is how it ends!”

Heather sighed, pinching the bridge of her nose. “You will pee again, Buddy. Just give it time.”

Neet sat a little way off while Heather steadied Buddy. For once, there was nothing useful he could announce.

Buddy sighed dramatically. “I am… a shadow of my former self.”

He looked at the bush, then at the door. One had welcomed him with twigs. The other had his blanket on the far side.

He leaned into Heather's hand and chose the door.

Neet followed, walking around the bush with unusual care.

A Very Ordinary Blanket

Heather settled Buddy at home. He tried putting his chin one way, then another. The blanket stayed reassuringly underneath him through both attempts.

Neet came over to check on him.

“You went right in,” he said.

Buddy opened an eye.

“Into the bush. All of you.”

“I was there.”

“I know. I was outside.”

Buddy shut the eye again. “Keep the story there.”

Neet looked toward Heather, who was sitting nearby. Then he looked at Buddy's tired face. For once, he let a remarkable event go unannounced.

Instead, he fetched Buddy's blue chew toy and set it beside the blanket.

Buddy raised his chin. The toy was still his. His brother had brought it over rather than carrying it away.

“Didn't inherit it, then?”

“You came back,” Neet said.

Buddy put a paw beside the toy, satisfied. He didn't need to do anything with it just now. Being here, with his things and his brother, was enough.

Heather stroked his head. “That's my Bud.”

He leaned toward her fingers. Apparently he could become elite and still be that.

The Big Realization

After an hour of resting, with a few unsteady attempts to get comfortable, Buddy started feeling more like himself. He licked his lips, still getting used to the weird sensation in his mouth.

Then, he caught a glimpse of himself in the mirror.

The light hit his shiny golden tooth.

His eyes widened.

“Wait a minute…” Buddy whispered. “I HAVE A GOLD TOOTH.”

Neet, who had begun recounting the bush incident, stopped halfway through it.

Buddy turned toward him slowly. “Neet. Did you know about this?”

Neet blinked. “Uh…”

Buddy shifted until the light caught the tooth again. “That is rather good.”

He looked back at the mirror. The shine disappeared when he turned too far. He adjusted his head and found it again. This time he stayed still long enough to enjoy it.

Then he turned the same smile on Neet. “You were saying something about the bush?”

“No.”

“You'd got to the part where all of me went in.”

Neet's ears flattened. “Oh no.”

Buddy trotted up to him smugly. “And you? You are a commoner now.”

Neet gasped. “NO!”

Buddy flicked his tail. “Bow before my golden tooth.”

Neet lowered his head a fraction, caught himself, and began sniffing the floor very busily.

“Dropped something,” he said.

“Your rank?” Buddy asked.

Neet growled. “I’LL GET ONE TOO!”

Buddy settled onto his blanket with the shining side of his mouth toward Neet. “Good luck.”

Heather, watching the whole exchange, groaned. “I have created a monster.”

She was smiling when Buddy looked up at her. She tucked the blanket around him, leaving his toy within reach.

Buddy closed his eyes, still smiling just enough to leave the gold showing. Neet moved to the other side of the room. The light was no better there.
