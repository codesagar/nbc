The Great Vet Escapade

Saturday mornings in the Patel kingdom usually meant pancakes, backyard patrols, and possibly a nap as long as the Mississippi River. But this Saturday had a different destiny.

“Time for the vet,” Sagar announced, slipping leashes into his hand like a magician revealing cards.

Neet’s ears perked. “The car? An expedition? Adventure awaits!”

Buddy bounced like a spring-loaded potato. “Where? Park? Chiku Shack? Ice cream factory?!”

Heather looked at the leashes, then at Sagar. “They heard the car part.”

Neither suspected the truth.

Buddy reached the front door first. This was a promising start. On outings, Neet usually chose the pace, the stops, and which harmless object urgently required interrogation. Today Buddy intended to get somewhere before the general discovered a reason to delay.

Neet stopped beside the umbrella stand.

“Possible concealed personnel.”

Buddy looked back. “It’s umbrellas.”

“A classic disguise.”

Sagar opened the door, and the investigation was abandoned in favor of immediate glory. Heather followed, taking the keys he offered without interrupting her smile.

“Everyone ready?” she asked.

Buddy certainly was. He would be an efficient dog today.

The Great Betrayal of the Threshold

The car ride began in pure euphoria. Neet sat tall, nose lifted to the wind, scanning the horizon like a commander inspecting his realm. Buddy pressed his whole face against the window, leaving foggy art and commentary: “Mail truck! Squirrel! Oooh, that hydrant smells heroic!”

Heather smiled knowingly from the passenger seat.

But then… the fateful arrival.

They reached the vet clinic. Sagar parked. The humans strolled toward the entrance as if nothing were amiss. Neet strutted confidently, chest puffed, paws tapping like a parade march.

He charged through the glass doors, tiny nub vibrating, announcing to the lobby: “General Neet has arrived to inspect your premises!”

And then.

The scent hit him. Disinfectant. Medicine. Fear. The Vet.

Neet froze mid-stride. His front half was in the lobby, but his back paws skidded into reverse like a cartoon character realizing he’d stepped into a mousetrap. His entire body did a 180° pivot back out the door, dragging Sagar an inch.

“Retreat! RETREAT!” Neet barked.

The receptionist chuckled. Heather covered her face, laughing.

Buddy trotted in clueless, tail wagging. “Oooh, cookies on the counter!”

Neet dug in harder. Buddy took one hopeful step forward. Between them, Sagar stood holding two very different opinions.

Sagar stopped where he was. Heather stepped beside Neet and offered her hand to sniff. He inspected it thoroughly. It smelled like Heather, which was difficult to enter into evidence against her.

“Come on, sweetheart. We’re right here.”

Neet crossed the threshold sideways, preserving the possibility that he had merely been blown in.

The Efficient Dog

Buddy had now recognized the building. The cookies remained an excellent feature, but the place had other departments. His new objective was to get this visit finished before Neet negotiated them into an overnight stay.

At the counter, the receptionist handed Sagar a clipboard. Buddy watched the pen move. At home, people wrote things down, stood up, and went somewhere. This seemed worth encouraging.

He sat perfectly straight beside Sagar. When Sagar shifted his feet, Buddy stood. Sagar wrote another line. Buddy sat again.

Heather glanced down. “Are you helping with the paperwork?”

Buddy raised his chin. Absolutely. Someone had to keep it moving.

Neet waited beneath her chair, contemplating his life choices. “The door is behind us. The plan is obvious.”

“The plan is finish,” Buddy said. “Then cookies. Then door.”

“Your plan has unnecessary stages.”

The clipboard returned to the counter. Buddy stood promptly and pointed his nose toward the entrance. Sagar sat beside Heather instead.

Buddy stared at the empty place where the pen had been.

There was more paperwork somewhere. There had to be.

Then a vet tech opened the inner door and called their names. Buddy trotted toward her before Neet could organize a committee.

“Look at you,” Heather said, following. “Someone’s eager.”

Buddy glanced back at his brother. First into the room, first finished. Neet could study the chair legs for as long as he liked.

The door closed behind them. Buddy looked around for the cookie counter.

He found the table instead.

The Whiner-in-Chief

While Neet sulked in the corner, Buddy was called to the table first for a simple nail trim.

“Just nails,” Heather whispered to reassure him.

Buddy instantly began whining as though he were about to donate both kidneys.

The tech paused to adjust the clippers. Buddy paused too, watching her hands.

She picked them up again. He resumed exactly where he had left off.

“Eeeeeehhhhhh… eeeeeeeehhhh… Is that all of them?” Buddy tried to count the untouched paws.

He offered the paw she had just finished.

“Other one, sweetheart,” said Heather.

Buddy offered it more politely.

This was his best paw now. It had a proven record. If everyone would agree to use it again, the remaining paws could remain private.

The tech waited, her clippers lowered. Buddy waited too. An efficient dog could be extremely patient about the right thing.

“Bud,” Heather said softly, resting a hand against him. “That one’s done.”

Buddy looked at her hand, then at his other paw. He put the finished one down. The next one was available, though he felt this should be recorded as a considerable concession.

Neet watched from the corner. “I’m a 4.75-star general. I shall be setting an example.”

Buddy sniffled, looking betrayed. “But the clippers are sharp! They look at me funny!”

The vet tech, seasoned and amused, clipped with swift precision. Buddy continued his operatic aria of suffering, while Neet arranged his face into an expression he hoped the receptionist would remember.

Buddy tried turning that expression into a request for assistance. Neet lifted his chin a little higher.

No help there.

By the last paw, Buddy had stopped trying to substitute the first. It hadn’t shortened anything. He watched Heather’s face instead of the clippers and supplied a much smaller final whine, for the record.

“All done, Buddy,” she said.

He looked at every paw before believing her.

On the floor again, he headed straight toward the closed door. Sagar remained beside the table. Heather remained beside Sagar. Neet was being lifted up.

Buddy sighed. First finished had not meant first out.

The Rabies Affair

Next up: Neet’s rabies shot.

Despite his protests at the threshold, when placed on the exam table, Neet straightened his back like a soldier at inspection. His eyes locked on Sagar: Commander, if I fall, tell my story with honor.

The vet calmly administered the shot. Neet didn’t even flinch. But Buddy gasped dramatically from the sidelines. “HE’S BEEN SPEARED!”

Neet huffed. “Minimal sting. Approved procedure. Next.”

The vet tech chuckled. “He’s very stoic.”

Heather nodded proudly. “He takes after Sagar.”

Sagar grinned. “Yeah, until it’s nail trim time.”

Neet had already begun accepting the compliment. He stopped halfway through a dignified nod.

Buddy moved away from the door. This next part was worth staying for.

“Setting an example, are you?”

“I already have.”

Neet glanced down at the floor, preparing to descend into his new reputation. Sagar’s hand stayed beside him. The tech reached for the clippers.

The example was apparently not finished.

The Nail Trim Wars

Because now it was Neet’s turn.

The moment the clippers came out, the general’s composure cracked. His Zen evaporated like morning mist.

“Wait. WAIT. This was not in the treaty!” Neet barked, eyes bulging.

Buddy, still traumatized by his own manicure, sat beside Heather’s ankle and whispered gleefully, “See? Not so easy, General Tough Guy!”

Neet tucked one front paw behind the other. The tech waited. He changed which paw was in front.

“She knows how many you have,” Buddy said.

Neet looked toward Sagar for a ruling. Sagar rubbed the side of his neck, quiet and steady.

“You can be brave and still complain,” he murmured.

Neet found this interpretation surprisingly generous. He immediately lodged a complaint.

Buddy watched a paw emerge. He had been looking forward to the general’s loss of composure, but the clippers still looked exactly like clippers. He pressed closer to Heather’s ankle.

“The first paw twice doesn’t work,” he told Neet. “I’ve tested it.”

“I was concealing it.”

“That doesn’t work either.”

Sagar held Neet steady, murmuring calm assurances. Heather stroked his head. Buddy stopped gloating long enough to watch the clippers, then stayed close beside her.

Clip. Clip.

Each nail produced a grunt, a side-eye, or an indignant sigh. Neet turned his head away, then looked back to make sure his objection had reached the correct person.

Heather met his eyes without laughing at that one. “Nearly there, sweetheart.”

Buddy sat down. He could see the door from here. He could also see Heather’s hand moving gently over Neet’s head, just as it had rested beside him. Getting through the room took longer than getting into it.

For once, he let the humans set the pace.

But in the end, it was done. Neet hopped down, shaking his paws like a nobleman dusting off his sleeves after labor.

“Approved. But barely,” he muttered.

“All done,” Heather said.

Buddy checked Neet, the table, and Sagar. This time she seemed to mean everybody.

The Exit Parade

When all procedures were complete, the humans collected paperwork and treats. Buddy sat very straight beneath the cookie counter, offering his freshly trimmed paw to the receptionist.

Now that it was voluntary, he could have held it there all morning.

The receptionist reached for a biscuit. Buddy kept the paw up. Sagar reached for a pen.

Buddy looked between them. More writing. His morning had contained more signatures than walks.

Neet faced the exit, ready to march. “We have completed the mission.”

Buddy held his position. He had rushed into the wrong part of this building once. He would take his time in the right part.

The biscuit arrived. His paw came down.

“Very professional,” Heather said.

Buddy accepted the compliment with his mouth full.

Treat secured, Buddy strutted out the door, suddenly reborn, tail wagging: “See? I survived! I’m a hero!”

Neet followed with regal dignity, announcing to the waiting room, “Operation Vet completed. Casualties: none. Pride: slightly dented. Treats: acceptable.”

Back in the car, Buddy sprawled across Heather’s lap, already snoring. Neet, still upright, addressed Sagar solemnly.

“Commander, next time, please disclose the mission parameters before departure.”

Sagar chuckled. “If I did, you wouldn’t come.”

Neet blinked. “Correct.”

Epilogue

Back home, the generals flopped into their sunroom thrones, exhausted by their ordeals. Heather poured chai for Sagar, who leaned back in his chair, smiling at the comedy of the morning.

The dogs sighed in unison. Despite indignities and trimmed nails, they were safe, loved, and home.

Buddy woke long enough to inspect his paws. Neet pretended not to notice, then inspected his own.
