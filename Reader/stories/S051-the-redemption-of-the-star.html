A General in Exile

The demotion hung over the household like a thundercloud.

Neet had been stripped of his quarter star — reduced to a 4.25-star general — and in his heart, it felt like exile.

He no longer patrolled with confidence. Instead, he brooded in corners, sighing like a knight mourning a lost crown.

Buddy watched him pace the living room one evening and muttered, “You look like a Shakespeare play, but with fleas.”

Neet ignored him, eyes fixed on the King. Every move Sagar made — pouring tea, tying shoelaces, adjusting the thermostat — Neet watched for a sign that another tribunal might be called.

Sagar bent to tie a shoelace. Neet sat to receive judgment. Sagar tied the other one.

Buddy waited until both shoes were finished before lying down again.

He had a ball ready. He had brought it to Neet, who had inspected it without moving, and then to Heather, who had rolled it down the hall. Buddy returned with it to find Neet in exactly the same position.

“You missed an excellent ball,” Buddy said.

“Awaiting a decision.”

“About shoes?”

“About me.”

Sagar straightened and reached down to stroke Neet’s ears. Neet leaned into the touch, then sat upright again as soon as the hand withdrew. The verdict had evidently been postponed by affection.

Heather crouched to roll the ball once more. Buddy caught it, turned, and waited for Neet to pursue him. Neet was watching the thermostat.

Buddy carried the ball back himself. Apparently he would be playing both dogs.

Even a sunbeam couldn’t distract Neet for long. He wanted the star back, and he wanted to earn it in front of Sagar.

The Calm Before the Storm

Saturday dawned bright. Heather sat in the backyard with coffee, Buddy lounged belly-up in the grass, and Sagar leafed through his notes like the steady monarch he was.

Neet took up position near the deck stairs, still patrolling, but his movements were muted — the fire of pride dimmed to embers.

Buddy had already persuaded Heather to make three throws. The fourth had landed beside the deck, and he had decided to cool his stomach in the grass before retrieving it. There was no hurry. The ball was his, Heather was staying, and Saturday had barely begun.

Neet walked past it, paused, and sniffed toward the trash cans.

“Playing?” Buddy asked from his upside-down position.

“Checking.”

“The ball has been checked. Repeatedly.”

Neet continued toward the stairs. Buddy rolled over, retrieved his ball, and brought it up onto the deck. He put it beside the chair leg, where nobody could accidentally forget what the morning was for.

And then came the sound.

A metallic CLANG, followed by the unmistakable scritch-scritch of claws.

Heather jolted upright.

From the kingdom’s northern border — the trash cans — emerged a masked marauder.

The raccoon.

Thick-furred, beady-eyed, brazen. It clambered atop the lid, balancing like a circus acrobat, and looked straight into Heather’s eyes with insolence.

Heather froze. “Sagar…”

Buddy shot up and barked wildly: “SNACK BANDIT! STRIPED SNACK BANDIT!” before bolting behind a chair for cover.

Buddy leaned out for another warning bark. The raccoon looked at him. Buddy closed his mouth very slowly.

Neet’s head turned slowly. His eyes sharpened. His posture straightened. In that moment, the brooding knight vanished. What stood there was the General reborn.

The Battle of the Backyard

With no warning, Neet charged.

He streaked across the lawn toward the trash cans. The raccoon hissed, raised its claws, and stood its ground. For a split second, the world seemed to hold its breath — a standoff between knight and outlaw.

The clash was chaos:

  • The raccoon swiped; Neet ducked and countered with a bark so loud it rattled the fence slats.
  • It leapt down, aiming for the compost bucket, but Neet body-blocked it with the precision of a medieval shield wall.
  • They circled, locked in combat, dust rising from the battlefield (technically mulch, but the kingdom would remember it as dust).

Heather gasped from the porch, frozen with her mug in hand. She set it down carefully, missed the ledge, caught it, and put it down again without looking away.

Sagar rose at once and called Neet back. The general, having committed himself, scarcely seemed to hear.

Heather opened the back door. Sagar moved toward the stairs, keeping his voice low and firm.

“Neet. Here.”

Buddy crouched behind the chair. His ball lay against his forepaw. He looked at it, then at Neet. The general had refused it indoors. Perhaps an urgent invitation would work better.

“Catch this! Come back!”

Buddy shoved the ball out from behind the chair. It rolled across the deck, dropped down a step, bounced twice, and stopped in the mulch beside the compost bucket.

The raccoon turned its head.

Buddy withdrew his nose. He had delivered his favorite possession to the snack bandit.

Neet barked, and the raccoon swung back toward him. Buddy stared at the ball. Retrieving it now would involve being outdoors in a much more specific way than he wished.

Buddy peeked from behind the chair, eyes wide:

“Oh my God, he’s actually doing it. The lunatic is… heroic?”

Buddy moved to the other side of the chair for a better view. The raccoon turned. He changed sides again.

From this side he could see Heather at the open door, holding out a hand. Getting to her meant leaving the chair. Getting to the ball meant leaving the entire sensible portion of the yard.

“Buddy, come,” she said.

He took a breath and stepped out. Three strides across the exposed deck felt longer than every ball chase he had ever enjoyed. Heather’s hand met his chest, steadying him beside her knee.

The ball stayed in the mulch.

Buddy looked back at Neet. The general was facing the compost bucket, with the stairs behind him. From behind the chair, Buddy could only watch. Here, he could show him where to come.

“Neet! This way!”

One of Neet’s ears turned toward the deck. The rest of him remained fiercely occupied.

Sagar came off the bottom step, calling Neet and keeping the route back to the deck clear.

At last, with one final thunderous bark, Neet lunged. The raccoon faltered, scrambled back over the trash can, and fled over the fence, tail vanishing into the woods.

The backyard was still.

Neet stood tall, chest heaving, eyes fixed on the King.

Behind him, Buddy edged back toward the chair and gave the empty fence one firm bark.

“Stay out.”

Neet turned toward the ball in the mulch.

“Leave it,” Sagar said. He was at the bottom step now, one hand extended toward Neet. “Come here.”

Buddy could have gone after his ball. The raccoon was gone. So was every obstacle to the excellent Saturday he had planned.

Instead, he backed toward Heather and called, “We’re over here, General.”

Neet looked from the bucket to the stairs. Then he trotted to Sagar, who gathered him close before he could reconsider.

Buddy followed Heather inside without the ball.

The Return from the Front

Neet expected the announcement immediately. Sagar carried him to the rug, sat down, and inspected him instead.

The general presented a front paw with great solemnity.

“Thank you,” Sagar said, looking it over gently.

Neet presented the other. This appeared to be a ceremony with extensive paperwork.

Heather sat beside them and ran her fingers carefully over Neet’s short coat. He kept turning his head toward the back door until she put a hand beneath his chin.

“Let us see you first, brave boy.”

He finally held still. Buddy sat close enough for her free hand to rest against his shoulder. The deck had felt enormous without a chair around him. Here, he could breathe normally again.

Only after both dogs were settled did Sagar go back out. From behind the glass, Buddy watched him secure the trash-can lid, put the compost bucket away, and retrieve the ball from the mulch.

Sagar brought it inside and set it down before Buddy.

Buddy took it straight to the rug. He did not ask for a throw. The ball had been on a very disappointing expedition and needed to remain where he could see it.

Neet watched Sagar return to his notes.

“No tribunal?” he whispered.

“Apparently your paws came first,” Buddy said.

Neet glanced down at them. All present. All accounted for. He rested one beside Buddy’s ball.

For a little while, neither dog requested anything.

The Court Reconvenes

That evening, the tribunal gathered in the living room.

Heather recounted the tale, voice tinged with awe. “He scared it off. He protected me. Protected the house.”

Buddy added his own version: “It was terrifying. I mean, I was covering the rear flank from the porch— very important position. But yeah, the lunatic saved us.”

“From behind the chair,” Heather said, smiling at Buddy.

Buddy sat down. No need to dispute the geography.

He had brought the ball to the hearing. It rested between his paws, with a speck of mulch still pressed into its seam. He had considered leaving out the part where he sent it toward the raccoon.

Neet looked at him. “You called me back.”

“Eventually.”

“You were standing by the door.”

“Also eventually.”

Buddy nudged the ball once, then stopped it with a paw.

“Before that, I tried to bring you back with this. It went the wrong way.”

Neet regarded the ball. “Poor deployment.”

“Extremely.”

Heather reached down to rub Buddy’s chest. “You came when I called. That helped.”

Buddy let his weight settle against her hand. His contribution was smaller than the version he had been preparing, but considerably more accurate.

Neet sat before them, posture perfect, gaze unwavering. His fur still carried the scent of battle, his chest puffed with the quiet pride of a knight who had stood against the odds.

A small piece of mulch fell from his chest onto the rug. Neet kept his eyes on Sagar. Buddy looked at the mulch, then decided this was not the moment.

And then came the silence.

Sagar, the King, looked down at him. The seconds stretched into eternities.

Neet’s ears twitched. His paws trembled. His whole being screamed: “Say it. Approve me. Restore me.”

Finally, Sagar spoke, his voice even but carrying the weight of a royal decree:

“The tribunal acknowledges the General’s bravery. His honor is restored. The star… returns.”

Heather clapped softly, smiling. Buddy dropped his jaw in exaggerated shock.

Sagar bent closer. “And next time, both of you come when we call. We’ll handle the visitor.”

Neet stared up at him, listening now. Heather’s hand rested warmly between his ears; Sagar’s voice had softened. Nobody was asking him to patrol anything.

Neet held still until Sagar reached down. Then he pressed his head firmly into the waiting hand. He was once again 4.75 stars.

The Coronation of Silence

That night, Neet sat on the deck, head high, gazing out across the kingdom he had defended. The crickets sang. The stars shimmered.

Buddy flopped beside him, licking his paw, and muttered:

“So… you’re a hero now. Still a lunatic, but a heroic lunatic.”

Neet shifted over to make room. Buddy settled, keeping the chair within a convenient distance.

“Good position?” Neet asked.

“Excellent.”

Buddy’s ball rested beneath the chair. Neet looked at it, then at Buddy.

“Shall I?”

Buddy considered a throw, the stairs, the mulch, and the unexpected obligations of a Saturday.

“Tomorrow. Toward the house.”

Neet settled beside him. Heather drew her feet up onto the chair, and Sagar reached over to pass her the blanket from its back. She spread it across her knees without disturbing either dog.

This time, Buddy stayed beside him.

The star was his once more. For tonight, the yard could manage without a patrol.
