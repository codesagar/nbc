The Business Class Upgrade Disaster

10 minute read · 36 illustrations
In this story

  2. The Gourmet Meal Incident
  3. The Nap Chaos
  4. Enough Luxury
  5. The Final Disaster

After the unforgettable airport lounge experience, Heather, Neet, and Buddy finally approached the boarding gate. They were fully expecting a long, cramped flight home—until an unexpected announcement changed everything.

“Passengers Heather Richmond, Neet, and Buddy, please approach the desk for a seating update.”

Heather blinked. “Did we do something wrong?”

Neet grinned. “Maybe they finally realized I'm royalty.”

Buddy yawned. “Or security flagged your samosa crimes.”

As they approached the gate agent, she smiled brightly. “Congratulations! Due to an overbooked economy section, you've been upgraded to business class.”

Neet gasped dramatically. “You mean… like, the fancy seats? With the legroom?!”

Buddy's ears perked up. “Wait, actual space to stretch?”

Heather was still processing the miracle. “Wait… seriously?”

The agent nodded. “Enjoy your flight!”

Heather thanked her, then looked at the new seating assignments once more. Buddy watched her expression soften. Even she hadn't expected the airport to give something back.

Neet started walking faster.

“They've already given us the seats,” Buddy said. “You don't have to conquer them.”

“I should inspect them before anyone changes their mind.”

The Seats Are Too Nice

The moment they stepped into business class, Neet's jaw dropped. The seats were massive, reclining beds, and each had a built-in entertainment screen the size of a small television.

Neet ran up to one of the seats and jumped in. “Oh. My. Dog. Is this memory foam?!”

Buddy climbed into his seat cautiously, testing its softness. “It's so… plush.”

He stretched one foreleg, then the other. Neither reached the edge. He checked again.

From here he could see Neet across the aisle. He could also lie down without folding himself around somebody's bag. This was everything he had wanted, provided he arranged it correctly.

Heather plopped down into hers, sighing in bliss. “Okay, this is definitely not how I expected this flight to go.”

Buddy studied the little pictures on his controls. He pressed one, waited for the footrest to move, and took his paw away. Then he tested the backrest. One adjustment at a time.

Neet leaned out to watch.

“Which one is best?”

“The one where you stop.”

Neet started pressing random buttons on his own control panel. “Look, it moves! I am ascending!”

Buddy had worked out which button lowered the footrest and which one laid the seat flat. He wanted to remember them and then leave them alone. “Neet, don't touch every—”

Suddenly, Neet's seat fully flattened, sending him flipping backward. His paws flailed as he landed upside down, legs in the air.

A passing flight attendant stopped and stared. “Sir, are you… okay?”

Neet, still upside down, nodded. “Just… testing the luxury.”

Buddy took his paw off his own controls. He had found the setting called Enough.

Heather helped Neet turn over. The attendant returned his seat upright for takeoff, then helped Buddy with his.

Neet looked at the little chair picture on the panel.

“They should draw the dog upside down.”

“You've supplied the demonstration,” Buddy said.

Heather settled back, making sure both dogs were in place. Neet kept his paws together through takeoff. Buddy watched them until the plane climbed and the cabin began to relax.

The Gourmet Meal Incident

Soon after takeoff, the flight attendants began serving meals, and Neet's eyes widened when he saw the menu.

“This isn't… plane food. This is… fine dining.” He sniffed the air. “Is that… grilled salmon?!”

A tuxedoed attendant arrived, placing silver trays in front of them. Buddy marveled at his dish. “This steak looks real.”

He took a small bite. Then he stopped, so the next bite would still be there.

Neet stared at him. “What's wrong?”

“Nothing. That's why I'm taking my time.”

Heather picked up her neatly arranged dessert. “Is this… handmade tiramisu?” She took a bite and nearly cried. “I don't ever want to fly economy again.”

Meanwhile, Neet had abandoned all civility and was stuffing his face with everything in reach. “We eat like kings now!”

He finished the salmon before Buddy had reached his third bite, then glanced across the aisle at the steak.

Buddy drew his plate closer.

“There's more food here,” Neet pointed out.

“And more flight. You could wait.”

Waiting appeared to involve looking around for something else. Neet spotted the champagne glass on Heather's tray and got his paws around it.

“Neet, no,” Buddy warned. “That's not for dogs.”

Neet sniffed it. “It smells expensive. I must try it.”

Before Heather could stop him, Neet took a delicate sip… and immediately sneezed it all over the next passenger—a distinguished older gentleman wearing a tailored suit.

The man wiped his face slowly and looked down at Neet, who grinned sheepishly.

Heather offered him a napkin. Then she looked at his lapel and offered him the rest of the stack.

“Uh… cheers?” Neet offered.

Heather buried her face in her hands. “We're going to get downgraded mid-flight.”

Buddy looked from his unfinished steak to the man's sleeve. The napkins were already damp. He picked up his own clean napkin by a corner and held it toward Heather.

“Oh, thank you, Buddy.” She took it and passed it over. “I'm so sorry.”

The gentleman blotted his cuff. “I usually manage to spill it on myself.”

Neet sat taller, encouraged.

Heather moved the glass out of his reach. “That wasn't an invitation.”

The attendant removed it and brought a fresh napkin. Buddy waited while Heather wiped Neet's muzzle. His dinner was cooling, but the man had stopped looking at them as though they might sneeze again.

Neet glanced at Buddy's plate once more.

Buddy said nothing.

Neet lowered his eyes to his empty tray.

“I may have eaten too quickly.”

Heather finished cleaning up and finally returned to her tiramisu. Buddy took his next bite. Neet watched both of them with the patience of someone who had no food left to hurry.

The Nap Chaos

After the meal, the cabin lights dimmed, signaling quiet time. Heather laid Neet's seat flat, then stretched. “Finally, we can sleep properly on a plane.”

Buddy lowered his seat, choosing the setting he remembered, and curled up in the generous space. “I could stay here forever.”

He tucked one corner of his blanket under a paw. No drafts. No visible buffet. Nothing left to improve.

Neet, however, had different ideas.

“Wait. We have a BED on a PLANE,” he whispered excitedly.

Heather sighed. “Neet, just go to sleep.”

Neet flopped down and wriggled around. “But I can sleep any way. I can sleep sideways. Or upside down. Or diagonally!”

Buddy opened one eye. The aisle was still between them. A superb view of Neet was turning out to be less restful than he had hoped.

“Pick one,” he whispered. “Then keep it.”

Neet lay diagonally. His blanket twisted beneath his shoulder. He tugged it free and tried sideways.

Buddy closed his eye.

After about twenty seconds of stillness, Neet suddenly sat up. “Wait, what if I try sleeping like this—”

And with that, he rolled directly off the seat onto the aisle.

A passing flight attendant nearly tripped over him. “Sir, please remain in your seat.”

Buddy pushed his blanket aside and looked down at Neet in the aisle. “Heather. Floor again.”

Heather leaned over and lifted Neet back into his seat. Buddy reclaimed the blanket before it slid off his own bed.

Neet shook out his ears. His own blanket had rolled into a thick lump at the far end of the bed. He pawed at it, trying to flatten it while Heather settled back.

Buddy watched the lump move closer to the controls. He had no intention of hearing that motor again.

“Swap blankets?” he asked.

Neet stopped pawing. “Mine's bigger.”

“Mine's flat.”

Neet considered the offer. At present, his larger blanket was mostly a hill.

“Fine. But you have to bring it.”

Enough Luxury

Buddy stood, caught a loose corner in his teeth, and dragged his blanket off his seat. He stepped down into the aisle with it trailing behind him.

Heather opened her eyes.

“Now where are you going?”

Buddy stopped beside Neet's bed and put the corner down. Neet stared at the blanket, then at the suddenly bare seat across the aisle.

“No more experimenting once it's on,” Buddy said.

Neet looked down at the familiar blanket. “That depends on the quality.”

Heather smiled. “All right. Let me help.”

She removed Neet's crumpled blanket, shook it open, and laid it on Buddy's seat. Then she spread Buddy's blanket over Neet, keeping it clear of the controls.

Buddy climbed back onto his bed. The exchanged blanket covered more of him. He turned once, tucked his paws beneath it, and lowered his chin. A larger blanket and a quiet neighbor. He had negotiated well.

Neet lay with his nose just outside the smooth edge.

“This is a good position,” he murmured.

“Keep it,” Buddy said.

Heather rested her hand briefly on Neet's shoulder. She checked Buddy's new blanket and stroked his head before returning to her seat.

“Thank you, boys.”

The gentleman nearby dimmed his reading light. Neet's eyes closed. Heather's shoulders relaxed against her pillow.

Buddy stayed awake long enough to hear Neet's first small snore. It was much less troublesome than a button.

Then, at last, he slept too.

The Final Disaster

Just as they were beginning their descent, the flight attendant came by to collect any remaining items. Heather handed over her neatly folded blanket, then gathered the dogs' exchanged blankets. Buddy stretched and yawned, still in awe of the luxury.

Neet, however, had disappeared.

“Where did he—”

Buddy sat up. Neet's bed was bare. So was the aisle beside it.

Suddenly, a voice came from the cockpit area.

“Attention passengers, this is your captain speaking. We have clear skies and a smooth landing ahead… and also, there's a small dog attempting to push buttons.”

Heather gasped. “Neet?!”

Neet came sprinting back down the aisle, his tiny tail nub wiggling. “GUYS. I ALMOST FLEW THE PLANE.”

Buddy covered his face with his paws. “We are never getting upgraded again.”

Heather caught Neet beside his seat and lifted him in. “You have a perfectly good chair right here.”

“Theirs had more buttons.”

Buddy uncovered one eye. So that was what Enough had been competing with.

The attendant returned their seats upright. Heather kept Neet beside her through landing. When they taxied to the gate, he leaned back in his business-class seat, sighing in satisfaction.

“Best flight ever.”

Heather checked that he was still there. “Next time, we leave the buttons alone.”

Neet grinned. “Totally worth it.”

Buddy laid a paw over his own seat controls. They had reached the ground. He meant to stay in the chair.

At the gate, Heather came over to rub behind his ear. He leaned into her hand.

Across the aisle, Neet stretched one paw toward his panel, looked at her, and tucked it back.

For the last few minutes, all three of them enjoyed the upgrade.
