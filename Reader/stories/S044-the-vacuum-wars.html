The Vacuum Wars

Act I: The Calm Before the Roar

The morning after the Great Laundry Day Uprising, the guest blanket still had two dog-shaped hollows in it.

Heather was in the kitchen making her coffee. Sagar was at the dining table, sipping chai and scrolling through the news. Neet and Buddy were still lounging in their “morning mode”— Neet sprawled on the ottoman like a retired general, Buddy tucked in a tight cinnamon roll on the rug.

Buddy had spent yesterday pursuing towels that cooled and laundry that kept being carried away. Today he intended to stay in one comfortable place until somebody brought breakfast to it. The blue sock lay beside him. He rested his chin on it and closed his eyes.

Heather looked at the dog hair beneath the couch.

“Today… we vacuum.”

Neet’s head shot up. Buddy’s eyes went wide. The word had been spoken.

Sagar set down his chai. Neither dog took that as a good sign.

Act II: The Enemy Awakens

From the closet, Sagar retrieved The Machine— a towering, corded beast known to humans as “vacuum cleaner,” but to Neet and Buddy, it was The Roaring Serpent of Doom.

As soon as it emerged, Neet leapt off the ottoman and began a perimeter circle, barking rapid-fire alerts: “Intruder! Sound the alarm! This thing eats everything!”

Buddy backed toward the couch, taking the rescued blue sock with him. “I told you this day would come…”

“Take cover!” Neet ordered.

Buddy was already doing so. He objected to having a sensible decision turned into an order.

Buddy climbed onto the couch and put the sock against the back cushion. Heather folded the guest blanket over the arm, leaving room to sit. He dragged a little cushion in front of his sock and settled behind it. A raised bed. A screen. No more moving.

Neet hurried over.

“Command post.”

“Occupied.”

“I need a view.”

“There's an entire floor.”

Neet looked at the floor, where Sagar was uncoiling the cord. He decided to inspect the enemy instead.

Act III: Battle Formation

The moment Sagar plugged it in, the Roaring Serpent awoke with a deafening WHOOOOSH.

Neet, fearless in his role as 4.75-star general, charged straight at it. He darted in low, barking in its “face” (or what he assumed was its face) before retreating with the agility of a prizefighter.

Buddy stayed behind enemy lines, peeking over the couch like a soldier in the trenches. Occasionally he’d poke his head out, bark once, then duck back down. He waited for the vacuum to drown out the end of each bark. There was no need to give away his exact position.

Heather tried reasoning with them. “Guys, it’s just cleaning. We like clean floors.”

Neet ignored her. “Don’t talk to me about floors— this thing just ate a Cheerio that was rightfully mine!”

“It’s been there since yesterday,” Sagar said.

“I was saving it.”

Neet rushed to the dining chair where another crumb had fallen. He lowered his nose, opened his mouth—and Heather lifted the chair to let Sagar clean beneath it.

Neet stepped back. The crumb disappeared.

Now they were moving the scenery to assist it.

He looked toward Buddy, who had tucked his chin behind the cushion. Neet barked for reinforcements. Buddy raised one eyebrow above his defenses, saw that the kitchen was still several strides away, and lowered it again.

The sooner they cleaned over there, the longer they would leave his couch alone.

Act IV: The Great Kitchen Standoff

Sagar maneuvered the vacuum into the kitchen. This was Neet’s turf— the location of his food bowl, his water station, and countless crumb jackpots. He was not going to let the Serpent claim it.

For the next five minutes, Neet engaged in a dramatic back-and-forth with the machine:

  • The vacuum advanced— Neet retreated two steps, barking furiously.
  • The vacuum retreated— Neet advanced three steps, tail high, as if forcing it into surrender.

Heather lifted the food bowl onto the counter before Sagar reached its corner. Neet followed it with his eyes.

“Evacuation,” he told Buddy. “They're evacuating my breakfast.”

“The bowl was empty.”

“It was available.”

Heather set the water bowl beside it and wiped the little wet patch underneath. Neet inspected the missing bowls' places, then the machine. He had two more grievances and nowhere to drink them away.

Sagar paused to move a chair. Neet held his ground. The machine held its ground. Neet looked back at Buddy to make sure someone was witnessing the standoff.

The machine kept turning toward the living room. Buddy preferred it busy here.

Buddy checked that his sock was safely on the couch, then joined the fray. He kept clear of the machine and circled behind it, barking at its cord as if he were cutting off the supply lines. When Sagar turned, Buddy circled again and found himself back where he had started. He barked from there. A thorough encirclement could take time.

When Sagar drew the machine back, Neet turned toward Buddy to confirm that the retreat had been witnessed. He took a triumphant sideways step. His forepaw caught the slack cord on the floor.

His next step landed in entirely the wrong place.

Sagar switched the vacuum off and knelt. Neet was already upright, but Sagar gently checked his paw before gathering the cord clear of their feet.

Heather rested a hand against Neet's chest. “All right, little soldier?”

Neet stood very still for the inspection. Then he leaned into her hand a fraction, which he hoped looked like offering further evidence.

Buddy came close enough to sniff him.

“Did it get you?”

“An ambush.”

“You were looking at me.”

“Exactly. It knew.”

Heather returned both bowls to their clean places. Neet drank. Even a general needed to wet his throat before the next round of explaining.

Sagar took the machine back to the living room. Buddy hurried ahead, climbed onto the couch, and settled behind his cushion again.

The floor beneath him could be cleaned without his participation. This was the advantage of planning.

Then Sagar fitted the hose attachment and Heather lifted the cushion beside Buddy.

A small field of crumbs lay underneath.

Buddy stared. The couch had a floor inside it.

He caught the blue sock and pushed it into the gap behind the remaining back cushion. Heather's hand approached that cushion next.

Buddy placed one paw on it.

She waited. He placed his other paw beside the first.

“Sweetheart, I need to lift that.”

He pressed harder. If there were another floor behind it, he preferred not to know.

Neet arrived at the foot of the couch, looked up at Buddy's stance, and issued a sharp bark. “Hold your position!”

Heather gently raised the cushion from the other end. The sock slid into view. Buddy caught it before it reached the seat.

“Good catch,” she said.

He had defended a hiding place by showing her exactly which part to open. Yesterday's towel had at least required her to crouch.

The machine rumbled. Buddy stepped to the far end of the couch, sock in his mouth. Heather held out her hand to help him down.

Neet barked again. Buddy looked at his empty fortress, then at Heather. He went to her.

She sat on the dining chair while Sagar finished the couch. Buddy settled beside her feet, holding his sock. Her fingers found the soft place behind his ear.

The roar continued. Nothing was being taken out of his mouth. He loosened his jaw a little.

Neet trotted over.

“You've abandoned the position.”

“I've brought the important part.”

Heather let him stay while Sagar put the cushions back. Then Buddy went to inspect. His cushion was where he had left it. The guest blanket remained folded over the arm. There were no crumbs beneath the seat, which he was prepared to regard as somebody else's loss.

He lay down with the sock between his paws. Sagar moved toward the hallway. Buddy watched him go without following.

Neet hurried after the machine. There were still rooms it hadn't been warned about.

Act V: Tactical Retreat

After an hour of strategic vacuuming— during which Neet launched no fewer than 14 sneak attacks and Buddy executed 9 flanking maneuvers— the Serpent was finally silenced and returned to the closet.

Neet strutted around the house with the swagger of a commander returning from victory. “Once again, I have defended the realm.”

Buddy trotted after him. “Yeah, we defended the realm. You’re welcome.”

Heather let the dogs into the yard. Neet returned across the freshly vacuumed throw rug with muddy paws.

Buddy stopped at the edge and looked down the little brown trail.

Then he stepped around it. He wanted credit for this, too.

Heather picked up the rug without looking at Sagar.

“Shall I get the vacuum again?” he asked.

Both dogs turned.

“No,” Heather said. “I would like to have breakfast.”

She set the rug aside and fetched a damp towel. Neet took one step toward the kitchen.

“Wait.”

He stopped. Heather knelt and cupped a muddy paw. Neet lifted the other one, ready to leave, and discovered this only made it easier for her to clean that one next.

Sagar crouched beside them and steadied him while Heather worked. Neet let his weight rest against Sagar's hand. The floor was very slippery for a victorious army.

Buddy offered a spotless front paw.

“Yes,” Sagar said, giving it a brief touch. “We noticed.”

Buddy offered the other, in case the credit was being awarded individually.

Act VI: The Peace Banquet

To celebrate their “victory,” Sagar suggested a reward. Soon the kitchen smelled of scrambled eggs, toast, and chai.

Neet stationed himself by Sagar’s chair, still telling war stories under his breath. “And then I leapt at it, from this distance—”

Buddy cut in. “I saw it. You tripped over the cord.”

Neet paused. “The cord came at me.”

Heather laughed so hard she nearly spilled her tea. Buddy settled beside her chair, pleased to have contributed the part of the report Neet kept leaving out.

Sagar set aside small plain pieces of egg for them. Heather nudged his chai within reach while he divided the dogs' portions.

Neet ate his without interrupting the account of his courage. Buddy ate his before beginning an account at all.

“I also saved the sock.”

Neet swallowed. “Under my command.”

“You were on the floor.”

“Providing support.”

Buddy considered this. Neet had come when the cushion was being lifted. His advice had been useless, but he had come.

Buddy slid a little nearer Sagar's chair. “Next time, you can carry it.”

Neet looked at him, delighted to have been offered a trophy.

“While I rest,” Buddy added.

Neet returned his attention to the eggs. The appointment had unexpected duties.

Act VII: Lessons of the Day

By evening, the kingdom had returned to calm. Neet lay on the ottoman, head resting on his paws, watching the sunset glow through the windows. Buddy curled beside Heather, drifting toward sleep.

“Do you think they’ll ever stop overreacting to the vacuum?” Heather asked.

Sagar smirked. “Not a chance. The day Neet ignores the vacuum… the world will stop turning.”

Neet opened one eye. “You need me. That thing could rise again any day.”

And somewhere in the closet, the Roaring Serpent lay silent… plotting its next move.
