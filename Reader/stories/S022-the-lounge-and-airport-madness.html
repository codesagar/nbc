The Lounge and Airport Madness

9 minute read · 36 illustrations
In this story

  4. Lounge Showdown: Neet vs. The Fancy Businessman

After barely surviving security, Heather, Neet, and Buddy made their way to the airport lounge, looking for a moment of peace.

Neet, however, had other plans.

“Look at this place!” Neet barked, his eyes darting across the fancy leather chairs, complimentary food stations, and well-dressed travelers lounging with their steaming cups of chai. “It's paradise!”

Buddy chose a plush seat facing the departure display. He could rest and keep an eye on the flight without moving. “Wake me up when it's time to board.”

He tucked his paws under him. From here, the departure display was visible and the buffet was not. He considered this excellent planning.

Heather set her bags beside the chairs and eased her shoulders down. She put an empty cup on the little side table, intending to fill it once the bags were settled. Buddy watched her sit, then rested his chin. At last, everyone had somewhere to put themselves.

Neet had already locked onto his next mission: The Unlimited Buffet.

The Buffet Heist

Heather was busy finding space for her smaller bag when Neet spotted his first target—a steaming platter of freshly fried samosas. He licked his lips.

“Just one,” he whispered to himself.

Neet darted under tables and chairs, maneuvering past passengers with military precision. Buddy lifted his head at the patter of paws.

“Your seat is over here.”

Neet glanced back. “I'm checking the facilities.”

The facilities smelled wonderful. He slipped beside a low table where a waiter had set the samosa platter while rearranging the buffet. The nearest corner had practically invited him.

Just as he bit into a samosa, the waiter returned. Neet froze mid-chew.

“Is this one with you?” the waiter asked Heather. Neet stood beside the buffet with a half-eaten samosa dangling from his mouth.

Heather groaned. “Neet, drop it.”

Neet chomped down. “I can't. It's part of me now.”

Buddy lifted his head from his seat. “We've been here for five minutes.”

The waiter looked at the empty corner of the platter, then at Neet. Neet moved half a step sideways. The samosa came with him.

Heather knelt and held a napkin below his chin. Neet considered escaping beneath the table. Heather stayed where she was, her other hand resting lightly on his back.

He released the remains with the expression of someone surrendering a priceless heirloom.

“Thank you,” she said, to both him and the waiter.

The waiter lifted the platter out of reach. Neet followed it with his eyes.

“That's a terrible place for a buffet.”

Heather collected the fallen crumbs and apologized. The waiter accepted her apology, but his glance made it quite clear that a second inspection would end their visit.

Buddy watched her bring Neet back. The seat he had chosen couldn't see the buffet. This now seemed like a flaw.

The Recliner Debacle

After narrowly avoiding lounge expulsion, Neet returned, still munching on crumbs, while Buddy happily sprawled across an automated recliner.

“This chair moves!” Buddy barked excitedly, accidentally pressing the recline button with his paw. The chair tilted back abruptly, and Buddy yelped as he slid backward into full relaxation mode.

Buddy stayed rigid until the motor stopped. Then he lowered his chin.

“I meant to do that.”

“Oh wow, that actually looks nice,” Heather said, taking a seat beside him.

Buddy carefully moved his paw away from the buttons. One improvement was enough.

Then he checked the departure display. From his new angle, he could see the ceiling, a lamp, and the underside of his own eyebrows.

He craned his neck. Still ceiling.

Neet, jealous, attempted to jump onto a recliner of his own—but miscalculated. Instead of landing gracefully, he slid off the slippery leather and got wedged between two chairs.

“Traitors!” Neet howled, his legs flailing against the leather.

Buddy leaned over the edge of his recliner. “Heather. He's between the chairs.” Then he settled back, unable to suppress a quiet snort.

A passing flight attendant suppressed a giggle while Heather sighed, pulling Neet free before security had to be involved again.

She checked his paws and set him beside her chair. Neet shook himself, then inspected the gap from a safer distance.

“It was narrower than advertised.”

“It wasn't advertised,” Buddy said.

Buddy looked at the recliner controls. Raising the chair would restore his view, but the footrest was holding his legs at an extremely persuasive angle.

He left the controls alone.

The Unexpected Announcement

Just as Heather managed to regain control, an announcement blared over the airport intercom.

“Attention passengers: Flight AI-317 to New York has been delayed by two hours.”

Heather groaned. “Seriously?”

Neet, however, perked up. “Two more hours? That means—”

“No, Neet,” Heather interrupted. “No more buffet raids.”

Neet squinted. “Define ‘raid.’”

Buddy lifted his head far enough to glimpse the departure display, then settled his chin back down. Two hours. At last, a schedule he could work with.

Neet was already standing up.

“This is going to be a long delay,” Buddy said.

He needed Neet occupied. Something seated. Something that used his eyes instead of his teeth.

“Could you watch the room?” Buddy asked. “Tell us when people start leaving for the gate.”

Neet turned sharply. “A lookout?”

“A quiet lookout.”

Neet climbed onto an ordinary chair in the short row beside Heather. No buttons. No suspicious gap. He sat upright and surveyed the travelers with enormous seriousness.

Buddy closed his eyes.

For nearly a minute, his plan was magnificent.

Lounge Showdown: Neet vs. The Fancy Businessman

Bored with travelers who refused to leave, Neet decided to people-watch more closely. That's when he spotted a very fancy businessman in a sleek suit, sipping espresso while scrolling through his laptop.

A flash from the man's wrist crossed the leather beside Neet.

Neet squinted. “That guy thinks he's so cool.”

Buddy barely lifted his head. “Please don't.”

“I must assert dominance.”

“You must watch people leave.”

“He's distracting the lookout.”

In a single, calculated move, Neet hopped onto the chair right next to the businessman and stared at him intensely.

The man looked up, startled. “Uh… hello?”

Neet didn't blink. He held eye contact as if challenging the man to a silent battle.

The businessman, clearly uncomfortable, shifted in his seat. “Can I… help you?”

Neet finally blinked and barked. “Your watch is too shiny.”

The businessman drew his cuff over it. Neet sat taller.

Then the man lifted his espresso cup with the other hand. Neet discovered the cufflinks.

“There are two more.”

Buddy groaned. “Heather, can we board the plane early just to escape this embarrassment?”

Heather laughed but gently pulled Neet away before he started offering unsolicited business advice.

“Sorry. He's having a very exciting day.”

“I gathered,” said the man. He moved his espresso farther onto the table.

Buddy tried to sit up. The tilted cushion kept sliding him back. He had to stand to look properly apologetic.

Heather settled Neet in the short row of ordinary chairs beside her. One empty seat separated them. “You can watch from here.”

The businessman resumed typing. His cuffs flashed with every movement.

Neet leaned forward.

Buddy had appointed a lookout who now wanted to inspect jewelry. He could hardly pretend this was entirely Heather's problem.

He carefully climbed down from the recliner and walked over to the ordinary chairs.

Neet looked over. “Abandoning your post?”

“Changing the view.”

Buddy chose the seat between Heather and Neet. From here he could see the departure display, the buffet, and both of Neet's ears.

Neet tried to peer around him.

Buddy leaned the same way.

“This chair has an obstruction,” Neet said.

“Yes.”

The Last Attempt at Peace

Heather bent to gather her smaller bag from the floor. Buddy watched her rub the back of her neck before sitting again. She hadn't managed so much as a cup of water between collecting crumbs and extracting dogs.

She reached for her cup on the side table. It was empty.

“Stay here, boys. I'm just going to fill this.”

Neet immediately looked toward the buffet.

Buddy stepped down beside Heather before she moved. His coveted chair would be gone if someone took it. He glanced back at the soft cushion, then nudged her ankle.

“What, you too?” she asked, smiling. “All right. Together.”

Neet hurried after them. If both of them were going, he wasn't remaining behind to supervise an empty chair.

Buddy stopped beside the water station, well short of the food. Heather filled her cup. Neet studied a covered dish farther along the counter.

“No inspections,” Buddy said.

Neet looked at Heather. She was drinking. For once, no one was asking her to rescue anything.

He sat down.

Heather finished the water and looked at the two dogs waiting by her feet. “Thank you, ragamuffins.”

She took them back to their chairs. Buddy's cushion was still free. He climbed onto it and stayed upright until Heather sat down beside him.

Neet turned once on his own seat, lowered himself, and let out a long sigh.

“Hard work, looking out.”

Buddy touched his nose briefly to Neet's shoulder. “You're doing better sitting down.”

Heather rested a hand on each dog. The display stayed where it was. The businessman kept his cuff over his watch. No one touched a button.

For a little while, all three of them rested.

Final Boarding Call

After two long hours of chaos, lounging, and stolen snacks, their boarding call finally came.

Heather grabbed her bags. Buddy stretched lazily, pleased to have slept at last.

The waiter passed with a low basket of breadsticks, paused to clear the table, and discovered Neet standing beside him.

Neet selected one before the basket went up.

The waiter looked at Heather. She looked at Neet.

“We're leaving,” she said, with the weary confidence of someone who had already located the exit.

Neet, still holding onto the stolen breadstick, strutted toward the gate like he owned the entire airport. Buddy walked beside Heather, keeping him in view.

As they approached the plane, Neet put the breadstick down for a moment, glanced back toward the lounge, and sighed. “I'll miss this place.”

Buddy rolled his eyes. “The lounge won't miss you.”

Neet picked up his souvenir again.

At the aircraft door, Heather took the breadstick from him. “Let's give the next buffet a chance.”

Neet grinned. “Worth it.”

Buddy looked past them at the seats. He had plenty of lost sleep to recover.

This time, he would choose one facing Neet.
