# Writing Neet & Buddy

This is a concise working adaptation of the supplied character bible and writing guidelines. Those originals remain recoverable through [Recovery](Recovery.md); editorial compression introduces no owner-approved canon.

Write third-person comedy about Neet, Buddy, Sagar and Heather. The dogs drive the adventure through sensory discoveries, disproportionate reactions and their own choices. Keep humor throughout, brisk causal action, temporary tension and a positive ending rooted in family and home. The prose target is approximately 2,000–2,500 words, about ten minutes; existing shorter drafts remain historical texts. The supplied balance is roughly 70% humor/adventure, 20% heart and 10% romance, a tonal guide rather than a mechanical quota.

Neet's narrative voice is impulsive, intelligent, fiercely fearless and sometimes reckless. Ordinary stimuli become security missions for “General Neet” or the “4.9 star general.” The owner personality reference also gives him cozy, slightly timid rest and cute seriousness: choose and explain the episode interpretation when these conflict. Draw physical likeness from [Neet's photographs](../Characters/Neet/README.md).

Buddy is sensible, gentle and dryly witty, sometimes meek or intimidated, yet capable of bravery. Give him an independent want, attempt, choice and consequence. His relationships with each human are his own; his role cannot collapse into observing their romance. His quieter reactions and Neet's overdrive make comedy together. Draw from [Buddy's photographs](../Characters/Buddy/README.md).

Sagar is analytical, wise, compassionate and protective, expressing practical expertise and authority quietly. Heather is nurturing, grounded, playful and spiritually devoted, with a love of vibrant rainbow colors, glitter and sparkles. Their partnership is an established background of shared values, aligned thoughts, learning and comfortable companionship. The supplied private characterization includes Heather's childlike delight, deference and reverence toward Sagar, a fatherly psychological association and divine-partner association. These are authorial background only: show trust, guidance, comfort and small choices; never state those associations in story dialogue or exposition. Preserve the supplied specificity without turning the dogs' adventure into a romance or an overt royal allegory.

Use natural contractions, interruptions, inside jokes, comfortable silences and actions that imply connection. Keep King/Queen references extremely sparse and contextual. Buddy may call Neet “lunatic” when reckless; Sagar's “idiot” is deeply affectionate; Heather's “numbskulls” or “ragamuffins” is playful. Avoid nickname repetition, speeches and elaborate declarations. Dogs may talk to each other in narration while understanding human language only partially.

Begin with a concrete hook and situation. Build attempts, complication and an earned resolution. Every scene advances action or character; specificity, smells and sounds make the dog's perspective funny. End warmly, often with comfort or a final humorous beat. Home and being together are the recurring emotional truth; the original theme also associates that comfort with Sagar's presence.
